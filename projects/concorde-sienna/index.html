Concorde Sienna Overview

One tower, 260 homes, read from the K-RERA registration. Updated 12 October 2026.

One 29-floor tower between Kannuru Lake and Hennur Road

Concorde Sienna was registered with K-RERA on 22 June 2026 under PRM/KA/RERA/1251/446/PR/220626/008740. The filing describes one tower, Tower A: two basements, then ground plus 28 upper floors, 31 structural slabs and 91.5 m to the top, on 11,533 sq m of land. That is 2.85 acres, which the brochure rounds up to 3.

There are 260 homes, nine on each typical floor and eight on the ground floor, split as 58 registered 2 BHK, 58 registered 2.5 BHK and 144 registered 3 BHK. Super built-up sizes run from 1,200 sq ft for the 2 BHK to 1,870 sq ft for the largest 3 BHK, and the 10,000 sq ft Evolve clubhouse sits on the ground beside the pools.

Why the Tower Is a Long Slab The shape of the building follows from the shape of the plot. 11,533 sq m with 10,068 sq m left as open ground gives a footprint of only 1,464 sq m, and the key plan draws that footprint as a long, narrow slab rather than a square block. Nine homes hang off a spine with two lift cores, which is why every apartment reaches an outside wall on at least two sides. The trade is the corridor: a slab this long means a walk from the lift to the far units that a compact block would not have.
3 BHK or 2.5 BHK? Read the Plan, Not the Label The brochure sells units 05 and 08, at 1,552 sq ft super built-up, as a 3 BHK with two toilets. K-RERA has the same 58 homes registered as 2.5 BHK. Both describe the same home and the plan is published: three sleeping rooms, two toilets and 1,020 sq ft of carpet. Which word ends up on your agreement is worth asking before you sign.
Where the Project Stands Today The registration gives a project start date of 1 July 2026 and a proposed completion date of 27 July 2031, five years rather than the four a single tower of this size is usually quoted at. The promoter's first quarterly return, filed on 15 July 2026, recorded every floor at 0% complete, 0 of the 260 homes booked and nothing collected from purchasers. That is an early position rather than a bad one: a buyer entering now is at the very front of the queue, with the construction risk that goes with it and the full choice of inventory that comes with it.
Why Demand on This Corridor Is Diversified Demand sources on the Hennur Road belt are spread rather than concentrated in one employer cluster. Manyata Tech Park anchors the IT salary base to the south, the Hebbal-Nagawara employment node sits inside the same drive radius, the Yelahanka aerospace and defence cluster lies to the north, and Kempegowda International Airport draws aviation and hospitality households via the Hennur-Bagaluru Cross link. Single-tower supply on this stretch has historically been thin, as most launches here have been mid-rise gated communities.

All-In Budget, Not Just the Sticker Price Statutory charges sit on top of whatever the agreement value turns out to be. In Karnataka that is 5% stamp duty and 2% registration, so 7% of the agreement value, plus 5% GST on an under-construction purchase. On a ₹1.63 crore home that is roughly ₹11.4 lakh of stamp duty and registration and about ₹8.2 lakh of GST, taking the all-in outlay to about ₹1.83 crore before any interior spend.
Ask for Documents, Not a Brochure Because the registration is now live, the escrow account details, the allotment format and the agreement of sale draft all exist. Ask for them, along with the live price sheet and payment plan, before you pay a booking amount.
