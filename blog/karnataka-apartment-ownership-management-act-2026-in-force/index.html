Karnataka's Apartment Ownership Act 2026 Is Now Law

By the Luxura Habitat Team•November 1, 2026•7 Min Read

Back in September, we covered the Karnataka Apartment (Ownership and Management) Bill, 2026 after it cleared both houses of the state legislature, and flagged that it still needed the Governor's assent and a commencement step before it actually became enforceable law. That has now happened. The Governor gave assent to the Bill on October 9, 2026, and it was published in the Karnataka Gazette Extraordinary on October 15, 2026 as Karnataka Act No. 45 of 2026, according to a legal notice from Foxmandal. In plain terms: this is no longer a proposal working its way through government, it is now the law governing apartment ownership, resident associations and redevelopment across Karnataka.

1. What Actually Changed Between August and Now

The Assembly passed the Bill on August 21, 2026, and the Legislative Council passed it on August 24. Between then and the Governor's assent on October 9, the Bill existed in a kind of legal limbo: approved by the legislature, but without the force of law. That gap matters less for how the Act reads and more for whether anyone could actually rely on it. An apartment owners' association weighing a redevelopment proposal in September, for instance, could discuss the 75% consent framework the Bill proposed, but couldn't yet invoke it as settled law. With Gazette publication on October 15, that changes: the Act's provisions now have legal standing, even if some operational detail, such as how the new competent authority is staffed and begins hearing cases, may still roll out via further notifications.

2. The 75% Redevelopment Consent Rule, Now in Force

The centrepiece provision is unchanged from what we described in September, but it now carries the weight of enacted law rather than a passed Bill awaiting assent:

  • 75% owner consent for redevelopment. At least three-quarters of apartment owners in a complex must agree before that complex can be redeveloped, replacing the unanimous-consent deadlock that has stalled redevelopment of several ageing Bengaluru complexes for years.
  • A guaranteed payout for dissenting owners. Owners who don't consent are entitled to a settlement of no less than twice the market value of their unit, rather than being forced to accept redevelopment on any terms, or being left with no recourse at all.
  • A single resident association per project. The Act is understood to move Karnataka towards one association per complex, intended to reduce the fragmented or competing association structures that have complicated decision-making in some larger developments.
  • A dedicated competent authority for disputes. Complaints and enforcement now route to this authority rather than requiring a civil suit, with a first appeal available within 90 days of a decision and a second appeal within 30 days after that.

3. Why the Assent-and-Gazette Step Isn't Just Paperwork

It's tempting to treat the gap between legislative passage and Gazette notification as a formality, but it is the step that actually determines whether a provision can be cited, relied on or enforced. The old Karnataka Apartment Ownership Act and the Karnataka Ownership of Flats Act, both more than five decades old, technically remain the applicable framework right up until the new Act is formally notified and takes effect. Residents' associations that moved ahead on redevelopment discussions in September based on the Bill's provisions were, strictly speaking, planning around a framework that didn't yet legally exist. That changed on October 15, and it is worth being precise about that date if a redevelopment resolution, a dispute filing or an association restructuring hinges on which law applies.

4. What It Means If You're Buying or Already Own in Bengaluru

For owners in older complexes where redevelopment has been an informal conversation for years, the Act's enactment is the point where that conversation can turn into an actual resolution process, with a known consent threshold and a guaranteed minimum payout for anyone who doesn't want to participate. For buyers evaluating a newer project, the practical relevance is less about redevelopment, which is decades away for a recently launched development, and more about the broader legal clarity this brings to how associations are formed and disputes resolved, a modernisation that benefits any large gated community regardless of its age. Either way, since some implementation detail may still follow via rules or notifications, it's worth confirming the current operational status of the competent authority before relying on any specific enforcement timeline.

The Bottom Line

The Bill we wrote about in September has cleared its final two hurdles, the Governor's assent and Gazette publication, and is now Karnataka Act No. 45 of 2026: real, enforceable law rather than a pending proposal. The 75% redevelopment consent rule, the guaranteed dissenting-owner payout and the new competent authority are now the operative framework for apartment ownership across the state.
