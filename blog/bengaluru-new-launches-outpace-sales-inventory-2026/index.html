Bengaluru's New Launches Are Outpacing Sales: What Rising Inventory Means for Buyers

By the Luxura Habitat Team•October 21, 2026•8 Min Read

A PropNewz analysis published on October 9, 2026 took on a question that has been circulating among Bengaluru homebuyers this year: is the city's real estate market cooling off, or even heading for a correction? Pulling together data from Anarock, Knight Frank and other trackers, the piece's conclusion is a qualified no, sales and prices are both still rising, but it also lays out the real story underneath that question: Bengaluru's developers have been launching new homes faster than the market is absorbing them, and unsold inventory is climbing as a result.

1. The Numbers: Launches Up, Inventory Up, But So Are Sales

Anarock's data for the July-September 2026 quarter shows Bengaluru new launches rose about 17% year on year to roughly 17,720 units. Nationally, across India's seven largest cities, unsold inventory rose around 12% to about 6,30,590 units in the same quarter, as launches outpaced sales. That is happening in a market that is still growing, not shrinking: Knight Frank's H1 2026 data puts Bengaluru home sales at 27,968 units, up 5% year on year, the fastest growth rate among the eight cities it tracks, with the city's average price rising about 9% to roughly Rs 9,354 per sq ft. The picture, in short, is more supply than before, not less demand.

2. The Metric That Actually Signals a Slowdown: Quarters to Sell

The more useful number for a buyer trying to read this market isn't the inventory count itself, since a growing city should naturally carry more unsold stock in absolute terms, but how that stock compares to the pace of sales. Knight Frank tracks this as "quarters to sell," the number of quarters it would take to clear all existing unsold inventory at the current sales rate. In Bengaluru, this measure rose to 6.1 quarters in the July-September period, up from 5.8 a year earlier. That is a gradual softening in absorption speed, worth watching, but nowhere close to the kind of spike that typically precedes a genuine correction.

3. Where the New Supply Is Concentrated

Earlier in the year, JLL's Q2 2026 data had already flagged that new supply was concentrating heavily along specific corridors, Bellary Road, followed by Hosur Road and Whitefield, even as the quarter's overall sales dipped about 6% quarter on quarter amid a more cautious launch stance from developers. The pattern through Q3 suggests developers regained confidence and resumed launching at pace, which is consistent with inventory building up disproportionately in the city's most active new-launch belts rather than spreading evenly across Bengaluru.

  • More choice and more negotiating room, for now. A market with rising launches and a slowly lengthening quarters-to-sell figure typically gives buyers more projects to compare and somewhat more room to negotiate on price or payment terms than a tightly supplied market would.
  • Inventory doesn't clear evenly. Corridors with the heaviest new supply, like Bellary Road and Hosur Road this cycle, are the ones most likely to see softer near-term absorption, which cuts both ways: it can mean better deals, but it also means more competing inventory to filter through before picking the right project.
  • Price growth alongside rising inventory is the detail that matters most. If prices were falling while inventory piled up, that would be a genuine warning sign. Prices still rising about 9% year on year, even as supply grows, is a materially different and healthier pattern.

The Bottom Line

Bengaluru's 2026 story so far is one of a market absorbing more supply than before, not one losing demand. Sales are up, prices are up, and launches are up even faster, which is exactly the kind of imbalance that shows up as rising inventory without it meaning the market is turning. For buyers, the practical takeaway is to use the wider choice to your advantage, compare projects across corridors like Kanakapura Road and established North Bengaluru micro-markets, and lean on developer track record when a city has this much simultaneous new supply in play.
