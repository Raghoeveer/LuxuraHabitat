Bengaluru Business Corridor: BDA Set to Tender PRR Packages 2 and 3 by November

By the Luxura Habitat Team•November 5, 2026•7 Min Read

Reports from early October 2026 say the Bangalore Development Authority (BDA) is expected to invite tenders for Packages 2 and 3 of Bengaluru's long-pending Peripheral Ring Road (PRR), the project since rebranded the Bengaluru Business Corridor, by November 2026. The update follows the EPC tender already floated for Package 1, a roughly 20-km stretch between Tumakuru Road and Bellary Road estimated at Rs 3,348 crore under a Design-Build-Operate-Transfer (DBOT) model. For a project that has been approved, re-tendered and stalled more than once over the years, a fresh round of tendering is a real step, though not yet a construction guarantee.

1. Where Package 1 Actually Stands

The Package 1 tender covering the Tumakuru Road to Bellary Road stretch has already been floated, with the Bengaluru Business Corridor's chairman confirming the EPC process. But it isn't moving forward unopposed: a landowners' group has formally asked the BDA to recall the Package 1 tenders, arguing that land acquisition has not crossed the 80% threshold the project typically needs, that related cases are still pending before the Karnataka High Court, that environmental clearance has not been secured, and that the detailed project report itself is incomplete. We did not find a public BDA response to these specific objections in the reports reviewed, so this is an open dispute rather than a settled matter.

2. A Project With a History of False Starts

Context matters here. The PRR has been through at least three tendering attempts over the years, including an earlier Rs 27,000 crore public-private partnership structure that would have let a private concessionaire collect tolls for 50 years, an approach that was eventually shelved. The state cabinet has approved the project's roughly 117-km alignment at an estimated cost of about Rs 270 billion, and separately cleared Rs 4,500 crore for farmer compensation tied to land acquisition. Given that track record, a new tender for Packages 2 and 3 is worth watching, but it sits within a pattern of the project restarting rather than a confirmed, funded construction timeline.

  • Package 1 scope. Roughly 20 km from Tumakuru Road to Bellary Road, estimated at Rs 3,348 crore under a DBOT model.
  • Packages 2 and 3. Reported as likely to be tendered by November 2026, though exact routing and cost for these packages were not detailed in the reports we reviewed.
  • Open legal and clearance issues. Farmer objections, pending High Court matters and an incomplete environmental clearance process on Package 1 specifically.

3. What It Means for Buyers on the Tumkur and Bellary Road Corridors

The full ring is designed to link Tumkur Road, Bellary Road, Old Madras Road, Sarjapur Road, Kanakapura Road and other radial highways around Bengaluru's outskirts, cutting the need for traffic headed between, say, Nelamangala and Devanahalli to pass through the city centre. On the Tumkur Road side, that includes plotted developments such as KNS Samooha near Nelamangala, and on the Bellary Road side it touches the Devanahalli and airport-road belt where Sattva Vasanta Skye and other projects sit. That said, we'd treat this as a long-horizon positive rather than something to price into a purchase decision today. This specific ring road has missed timelines before, and a freshly floated tender, however welcome, is not the same as a committed completion date.

The Bottom Line

The Bengaluru Business Corridor's next packages appear to be moving toward tender, which keeps a long-delayed, strategically important ring road in motion. But Package 1 itself faces real objections over land acquisition and clearances, and the project's history argues for patience over optimism. For anyone evaluating a plot or apartment along the Tumkur Road or Bellary Road stretches, this is a development worth tracking rather than a reason to rush a decision.
