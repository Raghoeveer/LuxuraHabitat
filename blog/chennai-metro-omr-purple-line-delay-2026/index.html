Chennai Metro's OMR Line Now Targets December 2026: What It Means for IT Corridor Buyers

By the Luxura Habitat Team•November 11, 2026•8 Min Read

Chennai Metro's long-awaited OMR corridor, part of Phase 2's Corridor 3, has moved its target date again, and this time the shift is worth understanding in some detail because the corridor is actually being built in two separate pieces with two different outlooks. The roughly 20 km elevated stretch from Nehru Nagar in Perungudi down to Siruseri SIPCOT was originally meant to begin operations by June 2026. Current reporting now points to a December 2026 target, but only for the Nehru Nagar-Sholinganallur section; the remaining Sholinganallur-Siruseri SIPCOT stretch, awarded to Rail Vikas Nigam Limited (RVNL) under a contract reported at around Rs 1,134 crore, has faced its own separate construction disruptions and looks more realistically like a 2027 story. For anyone evaluating a home along Chennai's OMR IT corridor, this is useful, concrete information, provided you read it as two distinct timelines rather than one single metro opening date.

1. What's Actually Moving, and What Isn't

The two-contract structure matters because it means progress on one stretch doesn't tell you much about the other. The Nehru Nagar-Sholinganallur section, the northern half closer to the city, is the one currently associated with the December 2026 target, a meaningful six-month slip from the original plan but still the nearer-term piece of the corridor. The Sholinganallur-Siruseri SIPCOT section further south, which would extend service closer to the Siruseri IT park cluster, is the one that has run into reported construction disruptions on RVNL's contract, and separate CMRL and industry tracking points to that stretch opening closer to 2027. A broader Swarajya report from earlier in 2026 also referenced a target of around March 2027 for 40 km of elevated corridors across Chennai Metro Phase 2 as a whole, which is a useful reminder that the OMR stretch is one part of a much larger, multi-year construction programme rather than an isolated project running on its own clock.

2. A Pattern Familiar to OMR Watchers

This isn't the corridor's first slipped date, and OMR residents have been vocal about it: one widely circulated piece carried the plea "let metro run up to Siruseri," reflecting frustration among residents and commuters who have watched successive target dates come and go without the line actually opening to the stretch's southern end. That history is worth keeping in mind when reading any single new date, including the December 2026 figure in this piece: it should be treated as the latest reported target for one half of the corridor, not a confirmed, locked-in opening that buyers can plan around with certainty.

  • Two contracts, two timelines. Nehru Nagar-Sholinganallur (reported December 2026) and Sholinganallur-Siruseri SIPCOT (tracking closer to 2027) are separate pieces of construction, not one unified opening.
  • This corridor has slipped before. An original June 2026 target has already moved to December 2026 for just the northern half, so treat any date as provisional until CMRL confirms it.
  • The delay doesn't change any specific project's own facts. RERA status, pricing and possession dates for OMR projects are governed independently of the metro's construction schedule.

3. What It Means for Homebuyers Along the OMR Corridor

OMR's IT corridor, running through Perungudi, Thoraipakkam, Sholinganallur, Padur and down toward Siruseri and Egattur, already functions reasonably well on its existing mix of private vehicles, buses and ride-hailing, which is one reason the metro's delay hasn't stalled activity in the corridor's residential market. A completed metro line would still be a genuine long-term upgrade, adding a fixed-cost, traffic-independent commute option for the thousands of IT and ITES employees working along this stretch, but given the realistic December 2026 to 2027 window across the corridor's two sections, it isn't a near-term reason to pay a premium today. For projects this site covers in the corridor, such as Godrej Azure in Padur and the pre-launch Sobha Wisteria in Egattur, the metro is a relevant piece of long-horizon context for buyers planning to hold the property for several years, rather than a factor that should move a near-term purchase decision.

The more actionable takeaway for OMR buyers right now is to evaluate each project on its own independently verifiable facts, RERA registration, builder track record, price sheet and current road connectivity, and treat the metro's eventual arrival as a bonus that compounds over a multi-year holding period rather than as a reason to rush or delay a decision based on a construction timeline that has already moved once this year and could move again.

The Bottom Line

Chennai Metro's OMR corridor is making real, if uneven, progress: a December 2026 target for the Nehru Nagar-Sholinganallur section is a step forward from the original June 2026 plan, even as the Sholinganallur-Siruseri SIPCOT stretch continues to lag behind. For buyers evaluating Godrej Azure or other OMR corridor homes, it's worth tracking this timeline, but it shouldn't be the deciding factor in a purchase that should rest primarily on each project's own RERA status and fundamentals.
