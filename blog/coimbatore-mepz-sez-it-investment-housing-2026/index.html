Coimbatore's SEZ Investment Wave: What Rs 443 Crore and 2,400 New Jobs Mean for Housing Demand

By the Luxura Habitat Team•November 27, 2026•8 Min Read

Coimbatore's IT and industrial base kept expanding through 2026, with a fresh round of SEZ approvals adding both capital and jobs to the city. In August 2026, the MEPZ Unit Approval Committee cleared 9 new SEZ units and 2 export-oriented units, worth a combined Rs 443 crore in investment and an estimated 2,427 jobs. Separately, KGiSL's own SEZ for electronics hardware, software and IT-enabled services reportedly crossed Rs 50,000 crore in cumulative exports since inception, with the first 300,000 sq ft phase scheduled to go operational by September 2026. None of this is a real estate announcement, but sustained job creation of this kind is one of the more reliable long-run drivers of housing demand in a city, and it's worth unpacking for anyone tracking Coimbatore's residential market.

1. Who's Actually Setting Up, and Where

Two names stand out from the August 2026 approvals. ZF Industrial Technology is setting up a manufacturing facility at Aspen Park Infra Coimbatore, with a reported Rs 336.13 crore investment and 250 direct jobs, a sizeable single commitment for the city's industrial base. Visionet Systems is expanding at the ELCOT SEZ in Vilankurichi, adding Rs 11.31 crore in investment and 294 jobs. These sit alongside KGiSL's Vilankurichi-area SEZ, which has built up a Rs 50,000 crore export track record over the years and is bringing a new 300,000 sq ft phase online. Coimbatore's primary IT park at Vilankurichi itself spans 61.59 acres, giving a sense of scale for the area absorbing much of this growth.

This follows a pattern visible through late 2025 and into 2026: MEPZ approved roughly Rs 158 crore of investment and 2,700 jobs across eight units in one earlier round that included Suzlon Energy's Coimbatore unit, IBM India committed Rs 54.34 crore at Indialand Tech Park within KGiSL's SEZ for about 600 skilled positions, and Capgemini Technology committed Rs 75 crore for roughly 1,621 IT and ITeS jobs at the ELCOT SEZ in Vilankurichi. Read together, these approvals describe a steady, multi-year accumulation of IT and light-industrial employment concentrated specifically around the Vilankurichi and ELCOT SEZ belt, not a single one-off announcement.

2. Why Job Creation Matters More Than Any Single Project Launch

Individual project launches get more attention, but sustained employment growth in a specific locality is usually the more durable driver of housing demand over a multi-year horizon. Each confirmed job, whether at ZF Industrial Technology, Visionet Systems or the companies already established in Vilankurichi's SEZ ecosystem, represents a household that may need to rent or buy somewhere within a reasonable commute of that workplace. A city adding jobs in concentrated clusters, as Coimbatore has been doing around Vilankurichi specifically, tends to see demand build gradually in the residential micro-markets closest to that cluster, rather than uniformly across the city.

The practical implication for Coimbatore's housing market is that localities within reasonable commuting distance of the Vilankurichi-ELCOT corridor, rather than the city as a whole, are the ones most directly positioned to benefit from this specific wave of job creation. That is a meaningfully different, and more useful, lens than a generic "Coimbatore real estate is growing" framing.

3. What This Means for Coimbatore Buyers

We would not treat any single SEZ approval as a reason to pay a premium for a specific plot or project today. What this data does support is a reasonable, grounded thesis that Coimbatore's residential demand, particularly in localities with practical access to the Vilankurichi-ELCOT employment belt, has a genuine, job-driven tailwind behind it, distinct from purely speculative price appreciation. Buyers evaluating plotted developments in the city, such as Adissia New Meadows in Kovaipudur, Adissia Regal Arch in Neelambur, or Godrej Plots Coimbatore near the golf club area in South Coimbatore, should weigh each location's actual commute distance to this specific employment cluster as one input among several, alongside the project's own land title, RERA status, pricing and the builder's track record.

The Bottom Line

Coimbatore added a confirmed Rs 443 crore and roughly 2,400 jobs in SEZ approvals in August 2026 alone, on top of a KGiSL SEZ that has already generated Rs 50,000 crore in cumulative exports, concentrated specifically around the Vilankurichi-ELCOT corridor. That is a genuine, job-driven demand signal for Coimbatore's residential market, best read as support for a multi-year view of localities near this employment cluster rather than a reason to act on any single project today.
