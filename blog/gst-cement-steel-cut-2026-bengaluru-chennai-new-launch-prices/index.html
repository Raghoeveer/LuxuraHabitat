GST 2.0's Cement & Steel Cut: Is It Actually Lowering New-Launch Prices in 2026?

By the Luxura Habitat Team•November 25, 2026•8 Min Read

It has now been just over a year since GST 2.0 cut the tax rate on cement from 28% to 18%, effective September 22, 2025, alongside smaller reductions on materials like certain granite and marble products. Cement typically accounts for close to a third of a residential project's raw material cost, so a 10-percentage-point rate cut on it was, on paper, one of the more consequential tax changes for Indian real estate in years. Crisil Intelligence projected the change would reduce overall construction costs by 3.5 to 4.5%, Savills put the range at 2.5% to 4.4% across asset classes, and Shapoorji Pallonji's real estate MD went further, suggesting reductions of up to 5%. More than a year on, with new launches continuing to come to market across Bengaluru and Chennai, the question worth asking isn't whether the tax rate changed, it clearly did, but whether that saving has actually shown up in the price a buyer is quoted today.

1. The Gap Between a Construction-Cost Cut and a Buyer's Price

A reduction in a developer's input costs and a reduction in a buyer's purchase price are two different things, connected only by the developer's decision on how much of the saving to pass through. EY's own analysis of the GST 2.0 changes flagged this explicitly: the benefit to apartment buyers depends entirely on whether a developer chooses to pass the saving on, rather than retaining it as improved margin or using it to absorb other rising costs, such as labour, land, or financing. One independent estimate based on live project data put the realistic price benefit to buyers at around 1 to 1.5%, a fraction of the full 3.5 to 5% construction-cost reduction the rate cut was projected to deliver. A separate analysis argued that buyers of self-built, independent houses are more likely to see the full benefit than buyers of apartments in large multi-unit developments, where the saving gets absorbed across far more variables in a developer's overall cost structure.

This isn't a reason to dismiss the GST 2.0 cut as meaningless, it is a real, structural reduction in one of the largest line items in any construction budget, but it is a reason to be precise about what it actually guarantees a buyer. It guarantees nothing automatically. It simply makes a price reduction more affordable for a developer to offer, if they choose to.

2. What This Means Practically for Bengaluru and Chennai Buyers

  • Treat "GST 2.0 benefit" marketing claims with the same scrutiny as any other discount claim. If a developer's sales team invokes GST 2.0 as a reason a price is attractive, ask specifically whether the current cost sheet was revised after September 22, 2025 to reflect the new cement rate, and by how much. A specific, dated answer is far more useful than a general assurance.
  • New launches priced after late 2025 should, in principle, already reflect the lower input cost. Any project whose cost sheet and pricing were finalised after the rate change took effect has had the opportunity to build the saving into its numbers from the start, whether or not it chose to pass the full amount on to the headline price.
  • Projects priced before September 2025 are a different case. A project launched or priced before the rate cut may see its margin improve on units still under construction, without that necessarily being reflected in a lower quoted price for a buyer booking today, since the price was already set before the input cost fell.
  • The saving is more visible in materials-heavy segments like plots and villas. Buyers of plotted developments who plan to self-build, or villa buyers whose construction cost is itemised more transparently, are generally better positioned to see and verify the actual cement cost reduction than apartment buyers comparing a bundled per-square-foot price.

3. A Reasonable Way to Use This Information

Rather than treating the GST 2.0 cut as a guaranteed discount owed to you, use it as a specific, fact-based question to put to any developer whose new launch you're evaluating. For apartment buyers comparing projects in high-rise corridors, such as North Bangalore developments like Century Astoria in Immencity or newer launches along similar corridors, it's reasonable to ask whether the quoted per-square-foot price reflects post-rate-cut input costs, and to weigh that answer alongside the project's other fundamentals, location, RERA status, builder track record, rather than as a standalone reason to buy. For Chennai buyers evaluating projects on the OMR corridor or in West Chennai, the same question applies equally; the GST 2.0 rate cut was a national change, not a city-specific one, so there's no reason the pass-through pattern should differ meaningfully between the two cities.

The Bottom Line

GST 2.0's cut to cement's GST rate, from 28% to 18%, was a genuine, material reduction in the cost of building a home in India, but a year of real-world evidence suggests the pass-through to apartment buyers specifically has been partial at best, closer to 1 to 1.5% in practice than the full 3.5 to 5% construction-cost saving the policy made possible. If a developer's pricing leans on GST 2.0 as a selling point, ask for the specifics rather than taking the framing at face value.
