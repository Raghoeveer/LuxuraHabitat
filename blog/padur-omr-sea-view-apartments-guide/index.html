Padur, OMR: Sea-View Apartments Buying Guide

By Narayanan Rajesh•Published September 5, 2026•9 Min Read

Sea-view apartments on OMR are genuinely uncommon, most of Chennai's IT Corridor development faces inland, so when a project in Padur markets itself around sea-facing residences, it's worth understanding exactly what that means, what it's worth paying extra for, and how this specific stretch of OMR compares to the corridor's more established micro-markets. This guide covers Padur's location and connectivity, the IT-employment case for the area, what actually differentiates a sea-view apartment, and who this kind of purchase genuinely suits.

1. Where Exactly Is Padur?

Padur sits on Old Mahabalipuram Road (OMR), south of the more built-up Sholinganallur-Perungudi stretch that most people picture when they hear "OMR." OMR itself is Chennai's longest-established IT Corridor, home to a large share of the city's software and IT services employment since the early 2000s. Padur's position further down the corridor means it sits closer to newer IT employment clusters like the SIPCOT IT Park and Siruseri IT Park, while generally commanding a lower per-sqft entry price than the fully built-out sections of OMR closer to the city.

2. Connectivity and Employment: What's Actually Nearby

Unlike a pre-infrastructure growth corridor where catalysts are years away, Padur's core investment case rests on employment infrastructure that is already operating today:

  • SIPCOT IT Park: a large, established IT park a few kilometres from Padur, home to a significant concentration of software and technology companies.
  • Siruseri IT Park: another major, fully operational IT employment cluster within a short drive, one of OMR's larger IT SEZ developments.
  • Educational institutions: Hindustan University and schools like Sri Chaitanya are within a couple of kilometres, useful for families rather than only single professionals.
  • Healthcare: multiple hospitals sit within 1-2 km of Padur, covering day-to-day medical needs without a long drive.

The weaker points in Padur's connectivity profile are longer-distance transit: the nearest major bus terminus and Chennai's airport are both over 20 km away, meaning residents here are more dependent on personal vehicles or cabs for intercity or air travel than someone living closer to the city core. For a buyer whose daily commute is to SIPCOT or Siruseri, this trade-off is usually worth it; for someone who travels frequently by air, it's a genuine downside to weigh.

3. Padur vs. the Rest of OMR: A Quick Comparison

OMR is not one uniform market, pricing and positioning shift meaningfully as you move along its length:

  • Central OMR (Sholinganallur, Perungudi, Thoraipakkam): the most built-out, most expensive stretch, closest to the earliest wave of IT-park development and the best social infrastructure (malls, established schools, hospitals).
  • Padur and further south: a newer growth pocket, generally more affordable per sqft than central OMR, with SIPCOT and Siruseri as the primary local employment anchors rather than the older Sholinganallur-Perungudi IT parks.
  • ECR (East Coast Road): Chennai's separate, more leisure-oriented coastal corridor, running parallel to OMR but generally commanding its own premium for standalone villas and weekend homes rather than daily-commute apartments.

A sea-facing apartment in Padur occupies an interesting middle position: it offers some of the coastal appeal associated with ECR, but within a working IT-corridor location and price band closer to OMR's own norms, rather than ECR's leisure-property premium.

4. What a Sea-View Apartment Actually Adds, and What to Check

A genuine sea view is a real amenity, better natural light, cooler prevailing breezes for parts of the year, and a view that simply isn't replicable once the surrounding land is built out. It's also one of the more subjective, easy-to-overpromise features in real estate marketing, so a few practical checks are worth making before you pay a premium for it:

  • Confirm the specific tower and floor: a "sea-view" claim on a project's marketing page often applies to specific towers or higher floors, not uniformly across every unit. Ask for your exact unit's projected sightline, ideally in writing, rather than assuming every apartment in the project qualifies.
  • Ask about future construction risk: a sea view that exists today can be partially or fully blocked by future construction on adjacent, currently-undeveloped land. Ask what, if anything, is planned or zoned on the seaward side of the project.
  • Factor in coastal maintenance: apartments closer to the coast are exposed to more salt-laden air than inland properties, which can accelerate wear on external fittings, railings, window frames and exterior paint over time. Ask the developer about the specific corrosion-resistant materials and maintenance schedule used, and budget for a somewhat higher long-term maintenance cost than an equivalent inland apartment.
  • Weigh the resale premium realistically: a genuine, unobstructed sea view does tend to command a resale premium in most coastal markets, but that premium is usually specific to the exact unit and floor, not the project as a whole. Don't assume every unit in a "sea-view" project will resell at the same premium.

5. Who This Kind of Purchase Suits

A sea-view apartment on Padur, OMR suits a buyer who genuinely values the coastal outlook as a lifestyle feature and is willing to pay a premium for it, while still wanting the practical benefits of an established IT-corridor location for daily commuting. It's a strong fit for a working professional or family employed at SIPCOT or Siruseri who wants more than a standard inland apartment and has the budget to absorb both the sea-view premium and somewhat higher long-term maintenance costs. It's a weaker fit for a purely budget-driven buyer, who would likely find better value per sqft in an inland Padur or wider OMR project, or for someone whose priority is minimising commute time to Chennai's airport or intercity bus terminus, both of which sit over 20 km away from this stretch.

6. Rental Demand and Yield Considerations

For a buyer weighing this as a rental investment rather than an end-user purchase, Padur's rental demand base is anchored by the same SIPCOT and Siruseri IT Park employment that drives the location's appeal generally. IT and ITES employees working at either park are a natural tenant pool for a well-located apartment here, and that demand tends to be steadier than a purely speculative growth corridor because it's tied to jobs that already exist rather than infrastructure that's still being built. A sea-view unit specifically can command a modest rental premium over an equivalent inland unit in the same building, though this premium is typically smaller in percentage terms for rentals than it is for resale pricing, since most tenants prioritise commute time and rent affordability over the view itself. If yield is your primary goal rather than personal use, an inland unit in the same project or a comparable nearby development may deliver a better rent-to-price ratio than paying a premium for the sea-facing configuration.

7. A Practical Pre-Purchase Checklist

Before signing anything, it's worth working through a short, concrete checklist specific to a coastal OMR purchase like this one:

  • Verify the project's RERA registration independently on the TNRERA portal, rather than trusting the number printed in a brochure.
  • Get your specific unit's floor and tower confirmed against the sea-view claim, ideally in writing, rather than relying on a general project-level marketing description.
  • Ask about zoning or planned development on the seaward side of the plot that could affect the view over the project's construction and post-handover timeline.
  • Request the specification sheet for exterior fittings, railings and window frames, and confirm what corrosion-resistant treatment, if any, is used for coastal exposure.
  • Compare the per-sqft price of the sea-facing configuration against an equivalent inland unit in the same project to understand exactly what premium you're paying for the view.
  • Time your site visit, where possible, for a period when you can actually observe the claimed sea view and general coastal conditions in person rather than relying solely on renders.

None of this is unique to Padur specifically, it's the same due diligence any buyer should apply to a coastal or view-premium property anywhere in India, but it's worth restating because the premium attached to a sea view makes getting these details wrong more expensive than it would be on a standard inland purchase.

Our Honest Assessment

Padur's core strength is that it doesn't require a speculative, multi-year infrastructure bet the way some of Chennai's newer growth corridors do, the SIPCOT and Siruseri IT Parks are already operating major employment hubs today, not proposed ones. The sea-view angle is a genuine, marketable differentiator on this part of OMR, but treat it the way you'd treat any premium feature: verify it applies to your specific unit, ask about future construction risk on the seaward side, and budget realistically for coastal maintenance rather than assuming it behaves exactly like an inland property.
