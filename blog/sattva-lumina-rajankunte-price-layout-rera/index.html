Sattva Lumina Yelahanka: Price, Floor Plans, RERA and Complete Buyer Guide

By Narayanan Rajesh, Luxura Habitat•Updated September 30, 2026•18 Min Read

Sattva Lumina is Sattva Group's 13-acre apartment township in Rajanukunte (also spelt Rajankunte), on the Yelahanka to Doddaballapura road in North Bangalore. It packs 1,549 homes into eight high-rise towers and, unusually for this corridor, starts with a Studio at about ₹38 Lakh before running up to a three-bathroom 3 BHK at about ₹1.44 Cr. We get asked about it more than almost any other Yelahanka launch, usually by people trying to decide whether its entry price is a genuine opening or a trade-off they will regret.

This guide is our attempt to answer that properly. We go through the developer, the location, the master plan line by line, every configuration with carpet areas and room sizes read directly off the floor plans, the real per-square-foot rate, the full cost of ownership, the possession timeline and the risks. The FAQ section at the end covers more than 30 questions buyers have put to us.

1. The developer: Sattva Group

Sattva Group started in 1986 as Salarpuria Sattva and is one of Bengaluru's longest-running developers, with close to four decades of residential, commercial office, plotted and integrated township work. Group collateral cites 140+ projects, over 80 million sq ft delivered and an A+ CRISIL rating, and its footprint now extends beyond Bangalore into Hyderabad, Pune, Kolkata and Goa. The RERA promoter entity for Lumina is listed as Trinayani Realtors Pvt. Ltd., which is common: large developers usually register each project through a separate company.

For a buyer, the practical value of a developer of this size is execution capacity. A 1,549-unit, eight-tower project needs steady cash flow and procurement strength for the four or so years it takes to build, and that is where a group with a long delivery record is less likely to stall than a first-time builder. It is not a guarantee, though, so treat the brand as a reason to evaluate carefully rather than a substitute for checking the project's own paperwork. Sattva is also active on the same corridor with Sattva Thippapura, a plotted development further up the Doddaballapura road.

2. Location: Rajanukunte on Doddaballapura Road

Rajanukunte sits north-west of Yelahanka on the road that runs to Doddaballapura. It is the kind of location North Bangalore buyers describe as "the next logical step": far enough from Hebbal to escape the worst traffic, close enough to Yelahanka for schools and hospitals, and a straight run to the airport. The area still has lakes and tree cover (the site render shows a lake beside the plot), and density is much lower than the city core.

3. The master plan, decoded

The plot is long and narrow, running roughly north from a single entry on the main road. Here is what the drawing tells you:

  • Eight wings, A to H. The legend shows three tower types: two towers of 2B+G+30 plus one of 2B+G+31 (type 1), four towers of 2B+G+30 (type 2) and one of 2B+G+30 (type 3). Wings A and B sit nearest the gate; wings G and H are at the far end.
  • Clubhouse at the front. The clubhouse and pool sit beside Wing B, close to the entry. Everyone passes it on the way in, which usually means it gets used more.
  • Single entry and exit. One access point simplifies security, but with 1,549 homes it will be busy at school and office hours. Ask how the gate and the approach road are designed to handle peak traffic.
  • Fire driveways. An 8 m fire driveway runs around the towers and a 10 m one near the entry. These double as internal roads, while most parking goes into the two basements.
  • Existing nala with a 30 m buffer. A natural drain (nala) crosses the site between the middle and far wings, with a 30 m no-build buffer on it. That buffer adds green space, but it also means some towers face it. Check which wings sit closest and how storm-water is handled.
  • Civic amenities parcel. A separate civic amenity block runs along the entry road, as required by planning rules.
  • Kharab land on the edges. The plan marks a kharab cart track and a kharab footpath at the boundaries: government land not part of the project. Nothing unusual, but it confirms the site boundary.
Density check: 1,549 homes on 12.83 acres is about 120 homes per acre. That is typical for a 30-storey project in Bengaluru and much denser than villa or mid-rise communities. The 70% open-space figure is achievable because the buildings go up, not out, but expect busy lifts and common areas at peak hours.

6. What you will actually pay: the all-in cost

The ticket price is not the cheque you write. For an under-construction home in Karnataka, add:

  • GST: 5% on under-construction homes (without input tax credit). Homes with carpet area up to 60 sq m in metro cities and a value up to ₹45 Lakh qualify as affordable housing at 1% GST. Lumina's studio (269 sq ft carpet, ₹38 to 42 Lakh) appears to fit that definition, so ask the developer to confirm the rate on your cost sheet.
  • Stamp duty: 5% for properties above ₹45 Lakh (3% between ₹21 and 45 Lakh), plus surcharge and cess, levied on the higher of agreement value and guidance value.
  • Registration: 2% of the property value (raised from 1% on 31 August 2025).
  • Other charges: car parking, floor-rise and preferred-location charges (PLC), clubhouse membership, maintenance deposit and corpus fund, BESCOM and water connection charges, and legal/documentation fees.

The co-working space deserves a mention: for a location that is some distance from the big tech parks, a place to work from home without working from the bedroom adds real value. With 1,549 homes, though, ask about pool and gym capacity, booking rules for the party spaces, and which amenities come with the first towers versus later phases.

8. Possession timeline and RERA

Lumina is registered with Karnataka RERA under PRM/KA/RERA/1251/472/PR/060924/007009, and its plan is approved by the Local Planning Authority. The registration number's date code (060924) suggests it was registered in September 2024.

There are two dates to keep separate. Marketing material quotes possession from 2027 onwards, which is the target for the first towers. The completion date committed in the RERA registration is January 2029, and that is the legally binding one: if the developer misses it without an approved extension, RERA entitles buyers to interest for the delay. For a multi-tower project, handover typically moves wing by wing, so the date that matters is the one for your tower, written into your agreement for sale.

We recommend a construction-linked payment plan so your payments track real progress, and checking the quarterly progress updates the developer must file on the Karnataka RERA portal.

9. Who Sattva Lumina suits, and who it doesn't

Good fit

  • First-time buyers who want a branded, gated community at a sub-₹1 Cr ticket.
  • Rental investors looking for studios and 1 BHKs near the airport belt and North Bangalore's employment hubs.
  • Families working in Yelahanka, the airport, aerospace parks or Bagmane who want a full clubhouse without paying Hebbal prices.
  • Multi-generational households that need the 3 BHK+3T's third bathroom.

Probably not the right fit

  • Buyers who need to move in before 2027 or who can't live with a 2029 completion risk.
  • People who want a quiet, low-density community; 120 homes per acre means busy common areas.
  • Daily commuters to central Bengaluru, Whitefield or Electronic City.
  • Buyers who want villa or plotted formats; see Surya Valencia (villas in Rajankunte) or Sattva Thippapura (plots) instead.

10. The investment view

Lumina's investment case has three parts. The first is ticket size: a sub-₹60 Lakh entry in a Sattva-branded township is rare in North Bangalore, and small units usually rent quickly near employment hubs. The second is corridor growth: Doddaballapura Road sits between Yelahanka and the aerospace, logistics and manufacturing investment clustering along the STRR, which supports steady rental demand. The third is liquidity: 2 BHKs in branded projects are the easiest homes to resell in this market.

Against that, supply is the main risk. With 1,549 homes delivering over roughly the same period, early resale and rental listings in the project will compete with each other, which can cap rents and resale premiums for the first few years. Investors should plan on a hold of five years or more and not count on a quick flip. For a wider view of the market, read our Bangalore price trends for 2026 and Yelahanka vs Yelahanka New Town comparisons.

11. Risks and watchpoints

  1. Timeline: a four-year build to January 2029 carries normal execution and approval risk. Track RERA progress filings.
  2. Density and the single gate: ask how peak-hour traffic at the entry and lift waiting times are planned for.
  3. The nala: check storm-water design and how close your wing is to the 30 m buffer.
  4. Carpet vs saleable area: compare prices on carpet area and confirm the carpet figure in the agreement.
  5. Phased amenities: get the amenity handover schedule in writing.
  6. Infrastructure dependence: metro and road upgrades are future events; don't pay today for all of them.
  7. Launch-phase pricing: offers change quickly. Ask for any offer to be printed on your stamped cost sheet.

13. Due-diligence checklist before you pay

  • Verify RERA PRM/KA/RERA/1251/472/PR/060924/007009 on the Karnataka RERA portal and download the registered plan and completion date.
  • Get the title deed, encumbrance certificate, land-conversion order and any joint development agreement reviewed by your own lawyer.
  • Ask for the stamped cost sheet with every charge listed, and the specification sheet.
  • Confirm the unit's type number, carpet area and balcony area in writing.
  • Get the tower-wise completion date and payment schedule into the agreement for sale.
  • Pay only by cheque or bank transfer to the developer's RERA project account.
  • Check which banks have pre-approved the project for home loans.
  • Drive your commute at peak hours and visit the site on a weekday.

Our honest assessment

Sattva Lumina's strongest case is breadth and access: five configurations from about ₹38 Lakh to ₹1.44 Cr, a developer with nearly four decades of delivery, a clubhouse that genuinely covers daily life, and a master plan that keeps most of the land open. For first-time buyers, rental investors and families working in the north, it is one of the more sensible value options on the Yelahanka side of the city.

The trade-offs are just as clear: high density, a single gate, a 2029 RERA completion date and a location that relies partly on future infrastructure. None of these is a deal-breaker if you go in knowing them. Compare on carpet area, budget for the full cost, pick your tower with the nala and the gate in mind, and get every date and number in writing. Do that, and Lumina is a solid shortlist candidate.
