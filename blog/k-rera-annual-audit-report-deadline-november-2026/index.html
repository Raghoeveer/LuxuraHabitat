K-RERA's November 15 Annual Audit Deadline: What It Means for Bengaluru Homebuyers

By the Luxura Habitat Team•November 15, 2026•7 Min Read

Today, November 15, 2026, is a regulatory deadline that most homebuyers never hear about, even though it affects every ongoing RERA-registered project in Karnataka. Under a K-RERA notice dated June 19, 2026, promoters across the state are required to file their annual audit report for the financial year 2025-26 by today, with late filing reported to risk penalties under the Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016. It is a quieter cousin of the October 15 quarterly disclosure deadline we covered last month, but in some ways it checks something more fundamental: where your money actually went.

What an "Annual Audit Report" Actually Verifies

RERA's standard framework requires promoters to park a prescribed share of the amounts they collect from buyers, 70% under the central Act, into a separate, project-specific escrow account rather than their general company account. Withdrawals from that account are meant to be limited to that project's own land and construction costs, and are supposed to track roughly in step with the percentage of work actually completed on site. To make that restriction real rather than merely written into the law, the promoter is required to have a practising chartered accountant, along with an engineer and an architect, certify that withdrawals matched construction progress, and to file that certification with the regulator within the prescribed window after each financial year closes. The annual audit report is that certification, and the K-RERA notice setting November 15, 2026 as this year's deadline is asking Karnataka's promoters to produce it for FY 2025-26.

How This Differs From the Quarterly Update Deadline

We wrote last month about K-RERA's October 15 quarterly disclosure deadline, which covers a project's running construction and sales status. The two filings sound similar but check different things. A quarterly update tells you how a project says it is progressing. The annual audit report is specifically a money-trail check on the escrow account, confirming that the funds withdrawn actually line up with the work certified as complete. Of the two, the audit report is arguably the more consequential for buyer protection, because diverted or misused construction funds, not simply slow paperwork, are one of the most common root causes behind stalled residential projects in India.

Why This Deadline Deserves Buyer Attention, Not Just Developer Attention

It is easy to treat a compliance notice like this as back-office housekeeping that only concerns the promoter and the regulator. For a buyer, though, a gap in a project's annual audit filing history is a more specific and more serious signal than a missed quarterly update, precisely because it touches the actual flow of money you have paid in, rather than just the cadence of status reports. A promoter that files its annual audit reports on time, year after year, is giving you concrete, checkable evidence that its escrow account is being run the way RERA intends. One that doesn't is not automatically in trouble, filings do get delayed for mundane administrative reasons too, but it is a gap worth asking a direct question about before you sign anything, rather than one worth ignoring because the project otherwise looks finished and polished in its marketing material.

How to Check a Project's Filing Status Yourself

Before booking into any RERA-registered project in Karnataka, search its RERA registration number on the Karnataka RERA portal (rera.karnataka.gov.in). The portal shows the registration's current status along with the quarterly and annual updates filed against it to date. If a project you are seriously considering shows a lapsed registration, a long gap since its last quarterly filing, or no annual audit report on record for a financial year that should already have one, that is worth raising directly with the promoter, the sales team, or your advisor before you put down any booking amount. This is a five-minute check against a portal that is open to the public, and it costs nothing beyond the time it takes to search a registration number.

What This Means If You're Evaluating Bengaluru Projects Right Now

If you are actively comparing projects this month, this is a good occasion to add a compliance check to your shortlist process alongside the usual price, location and configuration comparisons. We've recently looked at two specific projects through this same lens of recent, dated developments, Brigade Eternia in Yelahanka against this month's Blue Line metro trial-run milestone, and Sattva La Vita off Hennur Road against the accelerated Hennur-Bagalur Road upgrade, and in both cases we'd encourage the same habit: check the registration number on the portal yourself rather than relying solely on what a sales brochure or a listing site tells you. We've also written more broadly about where RERA enforcement is heading in our piece on RERA 2.0's QR codes and faster complaint resolution, which is useful background if this is the first time you're digging into a project's regulatory filings.

The Bottom Line

November 15 will not make front-page news, and most buyers will never notice that this deadline existed at all. But it is a real compliance requirement tied directly to how your booking amount and instalments are actually spent, and it is a far more useful thing to check than most of the glossy brochure claims that come with a new launch. Search the registration number, look at the filing history, and ask questions if something looks missing, before you commit, not after.
