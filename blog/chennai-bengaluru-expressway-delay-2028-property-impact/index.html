Chennai-Bengaluru Expressway Pushed to 2028: What the Delay Means for Property Along the Route

By the Luxura Habitat Team•September 18, 2026•7 Min Read

The Chennai-Bengaluru Expressway has missed its deadline again. According to a Deccan Herald report, the National Highways Authority of India (NHAI) has floated a fresh tender worth Rs 633.12 crore to complete the stalled 25 km Kanchipuram-Arakkonam stretch in Tamil Nadu, after the previous contractor exited the project in May 2025 citing financial difficulties. With this stretch only around 53% complete and a new contractor needing up to 18 months once appointed, officials now expect the full 258-km expressway to open sometime in early 2028, roughly a year later than the end-2026 target that was itself an extension of an earlier deadline.

1. What's Actually Done, and What Isn't

It's worth separating the parts of this project that are working from the one stretch that keeps slipping. The 71.7 km Karnataka portion of the expressway is complete and already open to traffic. Progress in Andhra Pradesh is also advanced, with the bulk of its 85.2 km stretch finished. The holdup is squarely in Tamil Nadu's 25 km Kanchipuram-Arakkonam package, where construction has been at a near-standstill for months after the original contractor, D B Jain, ran into financial trouble and NHAI terminated the contract. A separate Tamil Nadu package, along with a stretch in Andhra Pradesh, is still targeted for completion by around October 2026. Once the new Kanchipuram-Arakkonam contractor is in place, that specific segment is expected to take at least 12 more months to finish.

2. Why This Keeps Happening

This is the expressway's third publicly reported deadline miss, after earlier target dates of December 2025 and mid-2026 both came and went. The pattern behind each slip has been similar: land acquisition delays, the relocation of high-tension power lines, and now a contractor exit tied to rising input costs. For a project meant to cut the Chennai-Bengaluru road journey from 6 to 8 hours down to roughly 2.5 to 3 hours, the gap between announced timelines and ground reality has been a recurring theme for several years.

  • The Karnataka stretch is a known quantity. Its 71.7 km is complete and functioning, so connectivity gains on that side aren't hypothetical.
  • Tamil Nadu's Kanchipuram-Arakkonam package is the swing factor. Until NHAI's fresh tender is awarded and work resumes at pace, any completion date for this stretch should be treated as provisional.
  • A full end-to-end opening and a locally useful opening are different things. Sections that are already complete are usable today, even if the corridor as a whole isn't finished.

3. What It Means If You're Evaluating Property Near the Corridor

Towns near the Karnataka-Tamil Nadu border, such as Hosur and Attibele, sit close to where this expressway is meant to cross, and both already have functioning connectivity into Bengaluru via NH44 and the existing NH48 corridor, independent of this project. That existing connectivity is what's actually driving demand and pricing in these micro-markets today. The expressway, once complete, adds a faster long-distance link to Chennai on top of that, but it isn't the sole reason these areas work for commuters into Electronic City or South Bengaluru right now. Treat the 2028 timeline as a future upside to underwrite, not a deadline to plan a purchase around.

4. The Practical Takeaway

If a broker or listing is using "the expressway opens next year" as a reason to book quickly, this history of repeated delays is a useful reality check. Evaluate any project on the connectivity, employment access and pricing it offers today, in areas like Attibele and Electronic City South, and treat the expressway's eventual completion as a bonus rather than a load-bearing assumption in your investment thesis.
