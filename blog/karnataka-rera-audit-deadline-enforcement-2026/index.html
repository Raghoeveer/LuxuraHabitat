Karnataka RERA's November 15 Audit Deadline and New Enforcement Powers: What It Means for Bengaluru Homebuyers

By the Luxura Habitat Team•November 11, 2026•7 Min Read

Karnataka's real estate regulator has quietly tightened its grip on developer compliance this quarter, and the most immediate deadline lands this week. Under a K-RERA notice dated June 19, 2026, every registered developer in the state must submit its annual audit report for FY 2025-26, the certified account of how buyer payments were actually used, by November 15, 2026. That deadline arrives alongside two other developments worth knowing about if you're evaluating a Bengaluru purchase right now: a new enforcement Standard Operating Procedure K-RERA introduced on or around September 24, 2026, and a Karnataka Real Estate Appellate Tribunal ruling that even the Bengaluru Development Authority (BDA) counts as a "promoter" under the RERA Act for its own layouts. Taken together, these point to a regulator leaning harder into enforcement than it has in previous years, which is good news for buyer protection even when it's inconvenient for developers.

1. The November 15 Annual Audit Deadline, Explained

RERA requires registered developers to maintain a separate escrow account for each project and deposit at least 70% of buyer collections into it, to be drawn down only against certified construction costs for that specific project rather than diverted elsewhere. The annual audit report is the mechanism that verifies this actually happened: a chartered accountant certifies how the escrowed funds were used over the financial year, and the report is meant to be filed with the regulator as a matter of routine. K-RERA's own June 2026 notice fixed November 15, 2026 as this year's deadline for the FY 2025-26 filing, with late filing exposed to penalties under Section 60 of the RERA Act. The audit report itself is not usually something a buyer sees directly, but a developer's track record of filing on time, or not, is a reasonable proxy for how seriously it treats its other regulatory obligations.

2. K-RERA Is Leaning Harder Into Enforcement This Year

The November 15 deadline isn't happening in isolation. K-RERA has reported that it extended compliance deadlines on these audit filings multiple times in the past, but overall compliance stayed poor regardless, which is why the authority has now invoked its powers under Section 38(1) of the RERA Act to levy penalties on non-compliant promoters rather than continuing to extend grace periods indefinitely. Separately, a September 24, 2026 notice introduced a new Standard Operating Procedure specifically for compliance and enforcement of K-RERA's own orders, including recovery certificates, the mechanism by which the authority enforces compensation awarded to aggrieved buyers. That SOP followed a Karnataka High Court ruling on April 27, 2026, which held that recovery certificates issued by K-RERA must be acted on without delay, closing off a loophole where enforcement could otherwise drag on indefinitely after a buyer had already won their case.

  • Penalties, not just extensions, are now the stated response to non-compliance. That is a meaningful shift in posture from a regulator that has historically granted repeated grace periods.
  • Recovery certificates must now be acted on promptly. A buyer who wins a K-RERA order against a developer has a stronger practical path to actually collecting on it.
  • Even government bodies aren't exempt. The BDA-as-promoter ruling extends the same disclosure and fund-usage obligations to government layouts that already applied to private developers.

3. What This Means for Buyers Evaluating a Project Right Now

For Bengaluru homebuyers, none of this changes the price, configuration or possession date of any specific project, but it does change the practical value of checking a developer's compliance history before you pay a booking amount. A pattern of missed or repeatedly extended audit filings on the K-RERA portal, rera.karnataka.gov.in, is a reasonable warning sign, separate from how polished a project's marketing material looks. For projects on this site that are already RERA registered, developers with an established, multi-project track record of on-time filings are generally the safer bet precisely because regulatory discipline tends to be consistent across a builder's portfolio rather than isolated to one project. It's also worth noting that this tightened enforcement environment has no bearing on pre-RERA, pre-launch projects: those simply aren't registered yet, so none of these audit or enforcement mechanisms apply to them until a RERA number is actually filed, which is itself one more reason not to pay anything toward an unregistered project regardless of how advanced its environmental clearance or marketing collateral appears.

The appellate tribunal's decision on the BDA is arguably the most structurally significant piece of this quarter's RERA news, even though it will take time to show up in day-to-day buyer experience. Treating the BDA as a promoter under RERA means government-run layouts and sites are now formally subject to the same disclosure, escrow and timeline obligations private developers already carry, which closes a long-standing gap where buyers of government schemes had comparatively less regulatory recourse than buyers of private projects. For anyone weighing a BDA site allotment against a private developer's plotted layout in the same North Bengaluru corridor, that gap narrowing is a genuinely useful piece of context.

The Bottom Line

K-RERA's November 15 audit deadline is a routine compliance filing on paper, but it arrives this year alongside a new enforcement SOP, a High Court ruling forcing faster recovery-certificate action, and a tribunal decision that pulls even the BDA under RERA's buyer-protection umbrella. None of it changes any individual project's own facts, but together it's a reasonable signal that Karnataka's regulator is tightening rather than loosening its oversight, which is worth knowing whether you're comparing a registered project like Brigade Banashankari or a plotted development such as Sattva Ekatra in Doddaballapur.
