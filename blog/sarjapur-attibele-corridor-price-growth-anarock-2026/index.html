Sarjapur-Attibele Corridor Prices Up 71% Since 2022: What It Means for Shriram 107 SouthEast Buyers

By the Luxura Habitat Team•November 7, 2026•7 Min Read

A set of recent reports citing ANAROCK research, picked up by outlets including GeoSquare, Dwello and Tradebrains, puts average residential prices on Bengaluru's Sarjapur-Attibele corridor at roughly Rs 7,800 per sq.ft by the end of 2025, up from around Rs 4,568 per sq.ft in 2022. That works out to approximately 71% growth in three years, ahead of the roughly 63% rise reported for Bengaluru as a whole over the same period. The same coverage cites a forecast of a further 25% rise by 2028, which would put the corridor near Rs 9,730 per sq.ft. We have not seen ANAROCK's original report directly, only this secondary coverage, so treat the exact figures as indicative rather than final, and verify with your broker or the primary report before using them in any investment decision.

1. What the Numbers Actually Say

The headline comparison is straightforward: a corridor that was already seeing steady demand has apparently outpaced the broader Bengaluru market by roughly 8 percentage points of cumulative growth over three years. Attibele sits at the southern end of this corridor, along the Hosur Road axis, roughly 15 minutes from Electronic City by road. That proximity to one of Bengaluru's largest IT and ITES employment clusters is the most commonly cited reason for the area's demand, alongside expectations around the Peripheral Ring Road, the Satellite Town Ring Road and future Namma Metro extensions reaching further down this stretch.

It's worth being precise about what this data does and doesn't tell a buyer. A corridor-level average blends very different kinds of inventory, from older resale apartments to new project launches, and from plotted layouts to high-rise towers. It is a useful signal of direction, not a guarantee that any specific project or unit will appreciate at the same rate.

2. What's Actually Selling in Attibele Right Now

Within this corridor, Shriram 107 SouthEast is one of the active projects in Attibele itself. Per the project's own pricing page, Phase 3, the phase currently being marketed, offers 2 BHK apartments of approximately 698 to 752 sq.ft super built-up starting from Rs 65 Lakhs onwards, and 3 BHK apartments of approximately 914 sq.ft super built-up starting from Rs 80 Lakhs onwards. Phase 3 is registered under RERA number PRM/KA/RERA/1251/308/PR/140222/004708, with an expected possession of January 2027. Phases 1 and 2 on the same site are already complete and hold their Occupancy Certificates, under RERA numbers PRM/KA/RERA/1251/308/PR/181122/002156 and PRM/KA/RERA/1251/308/PR/190715/002689 respectively. As always, buyers should independently verify every RERA number on the Karnataka RERA portal before making any payment.

The development sits on a 19-acre footprint with 78% kept as open space, and includes an 18,000 sq.ft (G+2) clubhouse with a skating rink and lake-facing amenity zones, per the project's own page.

3. How to Read This If You're Evaluating Attibele

A fast-appreciating corridor cuts both ways for a buyer. On one hand, it's a reasonable signal that demand in the area has been real and sustained, not speculative noise. On the other, a corridor that has already moved 71% in three years is, by definition, less of a bargain today than it was in 2022, so the investment case now rests more on future infrastructure delivery and job growth than on simply buying where prices used to be low.

For a project like Shriram 107 SouthEast specifically, the more useful due-diligence questions are at the project level rather than the corridor level: is the phase you're buying into RERA-registered with a credible possession date, does the developer have a track record of delivering prior phases on the same site (here, Phases 1 and 2 are already delivered with Occupancy Certificates, which is a meaningful data point), and does the current price per sq.ft for the specific configuration you want still make sense against your budget and loan eligibility.

The Bottom Line

The Sarjapur-Attibele corridor's reported 71% price rise since 2022 is a useful piece of market context for anyone evaluating Attibele, including buyers looking at Shriram 107 SouthEast's currently selling Phase 3. It should inform the conversation, not replace the project-specific checks that matter most: RERA status, construction progress and the developer's delivery record on the same site.
