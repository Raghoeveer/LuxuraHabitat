Pallikaranai Marsh Buffer Zone Could Shrink 96%: What It Means for OMR Buyers

By the Luxura Habitat Team•October 19, 2026•7 Min Read

Tamil Nadu's State Wetlands Authority has proposed a much smaller interim buffer, or "Zone of Influence," around the Pallikaranai Ramsar wetland, cutting it from about 3,358 hectares to roughly 128.19 hectares, a reduction of around 96.2%. The revised proposal was presented before the Madras High Court on October 12, 2026, and remained under the court's consideration as of October 13, per reporting from Verified.RealEstate. This is a proposal, not a ruling, but it's a significant one for anyone tracking construction approvals in Chennai's southern OMR-adjacent suburbs.

1. What's Actually Being Proposed

The backstory starts with a National Green Tribunal order dated September 24, 2025, which directed the Chennai Metropolitan Development Authority (CMDA) to stop issuing new building approvals within the marsh's Ramsar boundary and its wider zone of influence, in the interest of protecting the wetland. CREDAI subsequently challenged the scope of that restriction before the Madras High Court. The State Wetlands Authority's new proposal, now before that same court, would shrink the affected zone by more than 96%, from thousands of hectares down to just over 128.

2. Why Pallikaranai Matters for OMR's Property Market

Pallikaranai Marsh sits inland of the Old Mahabalipuram Road (OMR) IT corridor, and its zone of influence touches areas like Perungudi, Semmancheri and Medavakkam, neighbourhoods that have seen steady residential development pressure given their proximity to OMR's office campuses. A construction-approval freeze that's been in place for over a year in these pockets has real consequences for anyone trying to get a new project approved there, and a 96% reduction in the restricted area, if it holds, would reopen approval pathways for a large share of that land.

3. What's Still Uncertain

It's worth being precise about what has and hasn't happened. The report is explicit that this should not be read as a confirmed lifting of construction restrictions: the proposal is still awaiting the Madras High Court's decision, and the protected Ramsar wetland itself has not been reduced or opened up. A smaller proposed zone of influence is not the same thing as the marsh's own protected boundary shrinking. Until the court rules, the practical, on-the-ground approval freeze in the affected pockets remains exactly what it was before this proposal surfaced.

4. What OMR Buyers Should Do Right Now

If you're looking at a project close to Pallikaranai Marsh itself, in Perungudi, Semmancheri or Medavakkam, ask the developer directly whether its CMDA building approval predates the NGT's September 2025 order, and don't treat this proposal as a green light until the Madras High Court actually rules on it. If you're evaluating property further south on OMR, in corridors like Padur, Siruseri or Navalur, this particular dispute isn't directly relevant since those areas sit outside the contested zone of influence and continue through the normal CMDA and TNRERA approval process. Projects like Godrej Azure in Padur, which carries its own TNRERA registration, aren't affected by the Pallikaranai restriction, but it's still worth independently verifying any OMR-area project's approvals rather than assuming location alone settles the question.

The Bottom Line

A 96% proposed reduction in the Pallikaranai Marsh buffer zone is a significant development to watch, but it's a proposal before the Madras High Court, not a decision. Buyers close to the marsh should wait for clarity rather than act on the headline, and buyers further south on OMR should know this particular restriction doesn't apply to their corridor in the first place.
