Manyata Tech Park's Continued Expansion: What It Means for Meenakshi High Life Buyers

By the Luxura Habitat Team•November 13, 2026•8 Min Read

Manyata Tech Park, North Bangalore's largest and most established IT employment hub, continues to show signs of active growth rather than maturity-stage stagnation. Reports earlier in 2026 described a proposed redevelopment of the park's E2 Block aimed at modernising office infrastructure and improving space efficiency, and the park has kept adding tenants through 2025 and 2026, including an 80,000 sq ft lease by workspace operator UrbanVault at Embassy Group's Manyata campus. For a park that already anchors one of Bangalore's densest clusters of global capability centres and IT services firms, continued redevelopment and leasing momentum is a meaningful signal for the residential micro-market built around it, and one project in particular sits close enough to feel the effect directly: Meenakshi High Life on Rachenahalli Main Road, roughly 1.5 km from the park.

1. What's Actually Happening at Manyata

Manyata Tech Park has gone through several development phases since it was first built out, and it isn't static today. The E2 Block redevelopment proposal, reported in February 2026, is aimed squarely at keeping the park's older blocks competitive against newer Grade-A stock elsewhere in the city, rather than letting them age out of the market. Separately, leasing activity has continued at a healthy pace, with UrbanVault's 80,000 sq ft commitment at the park being one of the more concrete, dated data points available. Taken together, these point to a tech park that is still actively managed and reinvested in, not one coasting on its original build quality from years ago.

2. Why This Matters for Rachenahalli Specifically

Manyata Tech Park's employment base is the single biggest demand driver for the residential corridors around it, Hebbal, Thanisandra, Hennur and Rachenahalli among them, and Rachenahalli in particular has built its entire residential investment case around proximity to this one employment hub. Meenakshi High Life's own project material positions the development specifically on that basis: a flagship high-rise offering on Rachenahalli Main Road, built around proximity to Manyata Tech Park, one of Bangalore's largest IT employment hubs. A tech park that keeps redeveloping and adding tenants, rather than one that's visibly aging, is directly relevant to the rental demand and long-term appreciation thesis for every project in this immediate catchment, including this one.

3. The Project Itself: A Quick Recap

Meenakshi High Life is a RERA-registered, under-construction development by Meenakshi Infraa Projects, spread across 3.7 acres with 313 units across two towers. It offers 2 BHK (1,096-1,292 sq ft), 2.5 BHK (approximately 1,265 sq ft), 3 BHK (1,466-1,711 sq ft) and 4 BHK (approximately 3,004 sq ft) configurations, at an average base rate of roughly ₹12,400-12,500 per sq ft. Starting prices run from ₹1.37 Cr for a 2 BHK up to ₹3.69 Cr for the largest 4 BHK layout. The project carries Karnataka RERA registration PRM/KA/RERA/1251/309/PR/200923/006268, with possession currently expected in August 2027.

4. A Lower Entry Point on an Established Corridor

One useful way to read this news is as validation of a specific positioning choice. Meenakshi High Life's base rate of ₹12,400-12,500 per sq ft undercuts most comparable RERA-registered launches on the established side of the Manyata belt, while still offering a fairly complete amenity set across its 313 units. If the park's own office infrastructure keeps being reinvested in rather than left to age, that supports the case for buying into this corridor at a relatively accessible entry price today, rather than waiting and potentially paying a premium once a redevelopment story like the E2 Block proposal is fully realised and more widely known among buyers.

It's worth being precise about what this news does and doesn't confirm. The E2 Block proposal was reported as a redevelopment plan, not a completed project, and UrbanVault's lease, while a genuine and dated transaction, is one tenant's decision rather than proof of park-wide demand. Treat both as supporting evidence for Manyata's continued relevance as an employment hub, not as a guarantee of a specific timeline or a specific rental yield for any one residential project nearby.

5. What It Means for Buyers Today

For an end-user or long-term investor evaluating Meenakshi High Life, the practical takeaway is straightforward: the core demand driver behind this corridor, Manyata Tech Park's employment base, shows no sign of winding down, and in fact continues to attract fresh capital investment and tenant commitments. That supports the project's rental and resale case over the medium term, particularly once possession arrives in August 2027 and the development can draw on walk-to-work demand from Manyata employees directly. As always, verify the current RERA status and construction progress independently before booking, and ask the developer for the latest payment schedule and any ongoing offers, since under-construction pricing typically moves as a project approaches completion.

6. Financing a Purchase at Today's Pricing

At Meenakshi High Life's entry price of ₹1.37 Cr for a 2 BHK, most lenders will finance up to 75-80% of the property's value, subject to income and credit profile, leaving the remaining 20-25% plus stamp duty, registration and any applicable GST on the under-construction portion to be arranged upfront. On an indicative 75% loan-to-value basis, that works out to a loan of roughly ₹1.03 Cr, and at a floating rate in the 8.5-9% range over a 20-year tenure, the EMI would come to approximately ₹89,000-91,000 per month, a planning estimate only, since actual rates and eligibility depend on the lender and your financial profile at the time of application. For the larger 3 BHK at ₹1.83 Cr, the same assumptions put the EMI in the ₹1.19-1.22 lakh range. Karnataka's stamp duty and registration charges add a further 6-7% on top of the base price for a property above ₹45 lakh, so budget for that separately rather than assuming it's absorbed into the quoted price.

7. The Broader Manyata Corridor

Meenakshi High Life isn't the only residential option riding this thesis. The wider Hebbal-Thanisandra-Hennur-Rachenahalli belt around Manyata Tech Park has seen sustained apartment launch activity over the past several years precisely because of this same employment anchor, and renewed investment inside the park itself is a tailwind for that entire micro-market, not just one project. Buyers comparing options along this belt should weigh each project's specific distance to the park, its RERA status and its pricing on a like-for-like basis, rather than assuming proximity alone settles the comparison.

The Bottom Line

Manyata Tech Park's E2 Block redevelopment proposal and its continued ability to land large-format leases like UrbanVault's are a reminder that Bangalore's oldest major IT park is still actively competing for tenants, not quietly declining. For buyers evaluating Meenakshi High Life, roughly 1.5 km from the park on Rachenahalli Main Road, that's a genuinely useful, if incremental, data point supporting the corridor's medium-term demand story, on top of the project's own RERA-registered status and comparatively accessible entry pricing.
