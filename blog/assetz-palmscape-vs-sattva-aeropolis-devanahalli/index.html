Assetz Palmscape vs Sattva Aeropolis: Which Devanahalli Project Fits You?

By Narayanan Rajesh•July 27, 2026•6 Min Read

Two of the most discussed projects in the Devanahalli real estate corridor cater to entirely different buyer profiles — Assetz Palmscape (a villa plotted development on IVC Road) and Sattva Aeropolis (a premium apartment complex near the airport). This guide will help you decide which fits your specific profile.

2. Design Philosophy

Assetz Palmscape: The Nature-First Plot

Assetz Palmscape is built around an ecological design philosophy. The project dedicates over 60% of its total land area to open spaces, including a Miyawaki forest — a dense, fast-growing native tree plantation that creates a genuine micro-ecosystem. The road network within the development is designed at 9-metre and 12-metre widths, ensuring the community never feels congested even as it matures. It is fundamentally designed for buyers who intend to build a custom home — their own design, their own timeline.

Sattva Aeropolis: The Airport Professional's Apartment

Sattva Aeropolis prioritises airport proximity and seamless urban living. It targets corporate professionals — particularly those in the aviation, aerospace, and logistics sectors — who need fast, reliable airport access and do not want to compromise on urban amenities. The apartment format provides a ready-to-occupy home with professional property management, making it highly suitable for both end-users and investors targeting rental income.

3. The Returns Question

As discussed in depth in our Devanahalli ROI analysis, the two products optimise for different return types. Palmscape buyers are making a pure land appreciation bet — which has delivered 20–26% CAGR in Devanahalli since 2022. Aeropolis buyers are making a blended bet on rental yield (3–4%) plus moderate capital appreciation (12–15% annually).

The Verdict

These are not competing products — they are complementary ones designed for fundamentally different investment theses. Assetz Palmscape wins for long-term capital preservation and NRI buyers.
