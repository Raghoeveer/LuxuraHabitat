Bellary Road, Yelahanka & Manyata Tech Park: Connectivity Guide

By Narayanan Rajesh•Published August 27, 2026•10 Min Read

"Yelahanka" gets used as shorthand for a huge stretch of North Bangalore, from the established New Town core near the air force station all the way down to the Bellary Road frontage closer to Hebbal. That's a problem if you're trying to actually plan a Manyata Tech Park commute, because those two ends of "Yelahanka" behave very differently in daily traffic. This guide breaks the corridor down landmark by landmark, using Bellary Road (NH-44) as the spine, so you can judge a specific address, not just a locality name on a brochure.

1. The Corridor, End to End

Bellary Road (NH-44) runs roughly north-south through this part of Bangalore, connecting Hebbal at the southern end to Yelahanka, then Rajanukunte and onward toward Doddaballapur and Devanahalli further north. Reading south to north, the corridor passes through a recognisable sequence of localities: Hebbal, then Sahakar Nagar and Vidyaranyapura just off the main road, then Kodigehalli and Sathanur closer to the highway itself, then the more established Yelahanka New Town, and finally Singanayakanahalli and Rajanukunte toward the outer edge before the road continues on to Chikkajala and Devanahalli.

Two things follow from that layout. First, "near Yelahanka" can mean anywhere along a corridor that's genuinely 15-20 km long end to end, so a commute time that's accurate for New Town proper can be badly wrong for an address closer to Hebbal, and vice versa. Second, the Hebbal-facing end of this corridor, where localities like Vidyaranyapura and Kodigehalli sit, is structurally closer to the Manyata Tech Park and Outer Ring Road job cluster than the more traditional heart of Yelahanka New Town is, even though both get marketed under the same "Yelahanka" umbrella.

The practical takeaway: an address closer to the Hebbal end of this corridor, such as the stretch near Vidyaranyapura and Kodigehalli, shortens the Manyata commute meaningfully compared to an address deep in Yelahanka New Town or further north toward Rajanukunte, at the cost of being somewhat further from the airport-facing amenities and social infrastructure that has built up around New Town itself over the years.

3. Employment Drivers Along the Corridor

Manyata Tech Park is the single largest organised employment cluster within reach of this corridor, hosting a mix of global IT and business-services occupiers and drawing a steady base of rental and resale demand from anyone commuting there daily. Hebbal itself has its own smaller but growing office and retail cluster around the flyover junction. Further north, the KIADB Aerospace Park and the broader Devanahalli business district add a second, airport-linked demand driver, though that catchment is more relevant to addresses closer to Yelahanka's northern end and Devanahalli than to the Hebbal-facing stretch this guide focuses on.

For a household with one commuter working at Manyata and another with more flexible or airport-corridor employment, the middle of this corridor, roughly the Vidyaranyapura-Kodigehalli-Sathanur stretch, can function as a reasonable compromise: closer to Manyata than New Town proper, while still retaining NH-44 access north toward the airport corridor if that becomes relevant later.

4. Social Infrastructure and Landmarks

This stretch of the corridor isn't purely a commuter pass-through; it has its own established social infrastructure. Kristu Jayanti (Deemed to be University) is a well-known institutional landmark sitting just off Bellary Road, drawing both student and staff housing demand independent of the IT commute. The area also has meaningful green cover for a North Bangalore corridor, including Avalahalli Forest, Abbigere Park, and Jarakabandi State Forest to the west, and Bagalur Bamboo Forest further along the corridor. Established residential neighbourhoods including Vidyaranyapura, Kodigehalli, Sahakar Nagar and Jalahalli round out the immediate social fabric, offering the kind of everyday retail, schooling and healthcare infrastructure that a purely new, greenfield corridor typically lacks in its early years.

Upcoming Infrastructure

Namma Metro's Blue Line and associated extensions are planned to improve connectivity along parts of this broader corridor over the coming years, and a suburban rail network has also been proposed for the wider North Bangalore region. Both are worth factoring into a long-term hold decision, but neither should be treated as operational today. We'd encourage buyers to base near-term commute planning strictly on current road-based drive times, and treat any metro or rail timeline as a future upside rather than a current fact, since infrastructure completion dates in Bangalore have historically slipped more often than not.

5. What This Means for a Buyer Comparing Addresses

If your priority is minimising a Manyata Tech Park commute, an address on the Hebbal-facing end of Bellary Road, closer to Vidyaranyapura and Kodigehalli than to Yelahanka New Town's traditional core, is the more defensible choice on pure drive-time grounds. If your priority is the more established retail, schooling and airport-corridor access that New Town proper and areas further north have built up over a longer period, that trade-off runs the other way. Neither answer is universally "better," they serve different commute and lifestyle priorities, and a project's marketing material describing itself simply as "Yelahanka" won't tell you which end of that 15-20 km corridor you're actually looking at. Always check the location map, not just the locality name.

6. Commute Cost and Resale Value, Not Just Drive Time

A shorter commute is worth translating into real numbers, not just minutes saved. A daily round trip of 45 minutes each way versus 30 minutes each way works out to roughly five extra hours a week, or close to 250 hours a year, spent in traffic rather than at home. For a Manyata-based dual-income household, that time cost is often a bigger practical factor than the price-per-square-foot difference between a Hebbal-facing address and one deeper into Yelahanka New Town, even though buyers tend to anchor almost entirely on the latter when comparing two listings.

Resale value follows a similar logic over a longer horizon. Addresses within a genuinely short commute of an established employment cluster like Manyata Tech Park have historically held rental demand more consistently through market cycles than addresses further from any single large employer, simply because the tenant and buyer pool draws from a bigger, steadier base. That doesn't mean every home near Manyata outperforms every home in central Yelahanka on appreciation, locality-specific factors like density, amenities and construction quality matter just as much, but commute distance to a proven employment hub is a genuine, durable input into long-term resale liquidity, not just a short-term convenience.

One more financing-adjacent point worth flagging: home loan eligibility calculations don't account for commute cost at all, a lender will approve the same loan-to-income ratio regardless of whether the underlying property adds thirty minutes or ninety minutes to a daily commute. That means the commute trade-off is entirely on the buyer to price in themselves when comparing two otherwise similar loan-eligible properties along this corridor, banks won't do that math for you, and a listing's own marketing copy has no incentive to either.

Our Honest Assessment

The Bellary Road corridor between Hebbal and Yelahanka is a genuinely useful commute compromise for anyone working at Manyata Tech Park who still wants NH-44 access, but it is not a single, uniform micro-market, and treating it as one is where a lot of buyer confusion starts. The southern, Hebbal-facing stretch (Vidyaranyapura, Kodigehalli, Sathanur) offers a meaningfully shorter Manyata commute than the traditional Yelahanka New Town core, at the cost of being somewhat further from New Town's more established social infrastructure. Upcoming metro and rail projects are a real long-term upside for the whole corridor, but they aren't operational yet and shouldn't be counted on for a near-term commute decision. Our recommendation for any buyer evaluating a project here is the same regardless of which end of the corridor it sits on: pull up the actual location pin, not just the marketing locality name, and time the commute to your specific workplace at a realistic peak-hour before treating any listed drive time as fact.
