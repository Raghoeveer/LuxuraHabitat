Sattva City Doddajala Review: Price, Floor Plans, Location and Our Verdict

By Narayanan Rajesh, Luxura Habitat•Published September 30, 2026•16 Min Read

Doddajala is not a name most Bangalore buyers know. It is a village on Airport Road, a few kilometres short of Kempegowda International Airport, that spent years in the shadow of Devanahalli, Yelahanka and the aerospace parks around it. Sattva City is the project most likely to change that. At 50 acres and 3,460 homes, it is one of the largest residential launches in North Bangalore this year, and it is betting that the airport will keep pulling jobs, roads and people north.

This review sets out what you actually get. We read the unit sizes and carpet areas straight off the builder's floor plans, work out the real per-square-foot rate and the all-in cost, go through the master plan and amenities, and are candid about the risks of buying into a six-year build about 40 km from MG Road. Whether you are looking for a home or an investment, the aim is to help you decide on the numbers.

1. The developer: Sattva Group

Sattva Group started in 1986 as Salarpuria Sattva and is one of Bengaluru's larger developers, with homes, office parks and hospitality projects across the city. In North Bangalore alone it is building Sattva Aeropolis, Sattva Park Cubix, Vasanta Skye and Sattva Lumina, so Sattva City is part of a deliberate bet on the airport corridor rather than a one-off.

A known brand does not remove execution risk on a project this size. Funding 3,460 homes over six years takes serious capital, and the quality of the final product depends on the site team as much as the brand. Before you book:

  • Check recent deliveries: ask for a list of Sattva's last few residential handovers with promised and actual possession dates.
  • Visit a finished project: talk to residents about build quality, snag handling and how maintenance was handed over.
  • Search the Karnataka RERA portal: look for complaints or extensions on the developer's past projects.

2. Location: the Airport Road trade-off

Sattva City sits at Meenakunte Hosur in Doddajala, opposite the KRN Complex and about 1 km from the Sadahalli toll plaza. That puts it inside the airport's orbit rather than Bangalore's urban core. It is the project's biggest strength and its biggest limitation, and which one matters more depends entirely on where you work.

Who the location works for

If you work at the airport, in the Aerospace SEZ or in the growing business zone around Devanahalli, an 8 km commute is rare in Bangalore. Frequent flyers swap a 90-minute cab ride from Whitefield or Sarjapur for a quarter of an hour. People who work from home get space, greenery and cleaner air at a price that would buy much less inside the city.

Who it doesn't work for

If your office is in the CBD, Koramangala, Indiranagar, on the Outer Ring Road or in Electronic City, a 40 km daily commute each way is not sustainable, and no clubhouse makes up for four hours in traffic. Doddajala's schools, hospitals and shops are also still thin, so for now you will lean on Devanahalli and Yelahanka. Our Devanahalli area guide and metro and STRR explainer cover the infrastructure pipeline. Bangalore's projects tend to arrive a few years later than announced, so treat them as upside, not a given.

3. Master plan and towers

The master plan spreads 13 towers across separate parcels, split by an internal boulevard and joined by a green spine that runs through the middle of the site with a lake, stream gardens, floating walkways and a waterfront amphitheatre. Each tower is 2B+G+16 to 2B+G+21, so the buildings are high-rise but not extreme, and parking sits in two basements, leaving the ground free for landscaping. That is how the project reaches its 84% open-space figure.

Scale is the whole point here. Most North Bangalore launches are 5 to 15 acres with 500 to 1,500 homes. At 50 acres, Sattva City can support things a smaller project cannot pay for, such as four clubhouses, a full sports precinct and on-site retail. The trade-off is that you are buying into a small town of perhaps 10,000 residents, and how well it is governed after handover will decide how it feels in ten years.

1 BHK: a rarity in a township this size

The 1,161 sq ft 1 BHK has a 14'1" x 12'0" bedroom, a 12'0" x 22'6" living and dining room, a separate utility and a balcony. It is large for a one-bedroom, which suits a single professional or a couple working near the airport, but its 705 sq ft carpet means you are paying for a lot of common area.

2 BHK and 2.5 BHK: the family entry point

The 2 BHK (16F) has a 22'1" x 13'0" living and dining room, a master bedroom of roughly 14 ft by 13 ft, two bathrooms and two balconies. The 2.5 BHK (33A) is actually a little smaller in saleable area but has more carpet (1,036 sq ft): it adds an 11'0" x 8'1" study and a ground-level sit-out with a 50 sq ft private garden. If you work from home, the 2.5 BHK gives you a real room to work in for less money.

3 BHK and 3.5 BHK: the volume homes

The 3 BHK (8B) has a 34'8" x 13'0" living and dining room, three bedrooms and three toilets. The 3.5 BHK (08C) is the same plan with a 9'7" x 12'5" study and a 218 sq ft extended terrace, which turns the balcony into outdoor living space. For a family that wants a guest room or an office, the extra 174 sq ft is good value.

4 BHK and the duplex penthouse

The 2,975 sq ft 4 BHK, in Blocks 01 to 04, has a 32'7" x 13'1" living and dining room, a 16'0" x 14'2" master bedroom with a walk-in wardrobe, four bathrooms, a puja room and a servant room. It is built for large or joint families. The P01 penthouse in Block 04 is a duplex with a family lounge, a home office and a large private open terrace above, aimed at buyers moving out of an independent house.

What the numbers tell you

The 4 BHK is the value pick. At ₹3.00 to 3.50 Cr for 2,975 sq ft, it works out to about ₹10,100 to ₹11,800 per sq ft of saleable area, or roughly ₹16,100 to ₹18,800 per sq ft of carpet. Developers often price big units more keenly because they sell slower, and that seems to be happening here.

The smaller units carry a premium for the township. Doddajala land is still cheaper than Bangalore's established corridors, and Sattva's pricing reflects what it expects the area to become, not what it is today. That is normal for a township launch, but it is also the risk: if the area develops more slowly than planned, early appreciation will be slower too.

Launch pricing is usually the lowest. Builders typically raise prices in steps as construction progresses. If Sattva City fits your needs, buying early is cheaper. If you are unsure, waiting a year costs a little more but lets you watch the construction pace.

7. Amenities: making sense of 200+

"200+ amenities" is a marketing number, and you will also see 250+ on some portals. Counts like this include every garden, seat and path, so the number matters less than what is on the list and how it will be maintained. On Sattva City's master plan, the legend runs to 133 ground-level items and 58 podium items. The ones that matter:

  • Sports: tennis, badminton, pickleball, paddle tennis, futsal, kabaddi and beach volleyball courts, a net cricket pitch, a skate park, a golf putting green, an outdoor gym and jogging and cycling trails.
  • Water: lap pools, a kids' pool, a lagoon-style pool with decks and a lake with a waterfront amphitheatre.
  • Green space: two central parks, a Miyawaki forest, a kitchen garden and orchard, a butterfly garden, a farm garden and floating walkways.
  • Community: 4 clubhouses (a main clubhouse plus satellite clubhouses), a temple, shops, food kiosks and a visitor centre.
  • Family: kids' and toddlers' play areas, an adventure park, a pet park, senior gardens with reflexology paths and meditation lawns.

Some third-party reviews also mention an Olympic-size pool, a creche and a health centre. We could not find these on the master plan legend, so if any of them matters to you, ask for it in writing.

The question to ask: who will run all this after handover? For 3,460 homes, a professional facility-management company is far safer than leaving it to a new residents' association. A lagoon pool and four clubhouses are expensive to maintain, and that will show up in your monthly charges.

8. Pros and cons

What works

  • 50-acre scale with 84% open space, rare in Bangalore
  • About 8 km from the airport, one of the shortest airport commutes of any large township
  • A genuine sports and green precinct, not just a pool and a gym
  • Wide range of homes, from a 1 BHK to a 6,266 sq ft penthouse
  • The 4 BHK is competitively priced per sq ft
  • RERA registered, with launch-stage pricing

What to weigh

  • About 40 km from MG Road, a real lifestyle constraint
  • Possession in February 2032, with capital locked in for six years
  • Doddajala's schools, hospitals and shopping are still limited
  • No resale history yet, so liquidity on exit is unproven
  • 3,460 homes will need strong professional management
  • Smaller units are priced ahead of today's local market

These points do not carry equal weight. Location comes first: if you do not work in North Bangalore or from home, stop here. The 2032 timeline comes second: you need the money and the patience to wait.

9. Investment view

Doddajala is early in its growth. As an illustration, not a forecast, here is what a ₹1.82 Cr 2 BHK would be worth at possession in 2032 at different annual growth rates:

Optimistic10%~₹3.22 Cr~77%

What could push it up: growing airport traffic, more jobs in the aerospace and airport business parks, limited competing township supply nearby, and road and metro upgrades along the corridor.

What could hold it back: Doddajala has no price history to benchmark against, infrastructure could arrive late, other large launches could flood the corridor with supply, and a distant location narrows the pool of future buyers.

Rental yield is modest. After possession, a 2 BHK might rent for ₹40,000 to ₹55,000 a month at today's airport-area rates, about 2.6% to 3.6% gross on the purchase price and less after maintenance and vacancy. Our Devanahalli ROI analysis goes deeper on appreciation against rent.

For a pure investor, 6% to 8% a year with little liquidity for six years is not dramatically better than equity funds or REITs. The case gets much stronger if you will live in it, because the space and township lifestyle are real benefits a spreadsheet does not capture.

10. Who should buy, and who should skip

Good fit

  • Airport and aerospace professionals: an 8 km commute and a self-contained township.
  • Remote workers who want space: the study and garden layouts, the green spine and the quiet are worth more than city proximity.
  • Families who like big-community living: kids' facilities, sports and a ready-made social circle at a scale no nearby project matches.
  • Long-horizon investors: ten years or more, comfortable with a 2032 handover.

Probably not the right fit

  • Daily commuters to central, east or south Bangalore: the drive will dominate your day.
  • Anyone who needs to move in before 2032: look at ready or near-ready projects instead.
  • Risk-averse buyers: a new launch in an untested micro-market, with a six-year build, is above-average risk.
  • Those who need mature infrastructure today: top schools, multi-specialty hospitals and malls are not yet within 5 km.

11. Due-diligence checklist before you pay

  1. Verify the RERA registration, approved plan and committed completion date on the Karnataka RERA portal.
  2. Get the floor plan for your exact unit, with saleable, carpet and balcony areas written down.
  3. Ask for a stamped cost sheet that lists GST, parking, deposits and every other charge.
  4. Confirm your tower's possession date and phase in the agreement for sale.
  5. Ask which amenities come in your phase, and get anything important in writing.
  6. Understand the maintenance model, and whether a professional facility manager will run the township.
  7. Have your own lawyer check the title, encumbrance certificate and land conversion.
  8. Drive your real commute at peak hour before you book.

Our verdict

Sattva City is a good project in a location that is still becoming what the brochure promises. The 50 acres, 84% open space, sports precinct and airport proximity are real and hard to find elsewhere in Bangalore. The 4 BHK in particular is well priced for what you get. Against that, Doddajala is 40 km from the city centre, the neighbourhood is still thin, and a six-year build asks a lot of patience.

If your life is anchored to North Bangalore, the airport or a home office, and you can wait until 2032, it deserves a serious look, ideally at launch pricing. If your commute runs south of Hebbal or you need a home sooner, the amenities will not make up for the location, and you should move on without regret.

Possession and legal

Yes. It is registered with Karnataka RERA under PRM/KA/RERA/1251/472/PR/270226/008494. The registration lists the promoter, the approved plan and the committed completion date.

RERA possession is February 2032. The project launched on 27 February 2026, so it is a roughly six-year build. Your tower-specific date should be written into the agreement for sale.

Under RERA, if the promoter misses the committed date without an approved extension, you can claim interest for the delay or withdraw with a refund and interest. Keep every receipt and your registered agreement.

RERA-registered projects from established developers are usually approved by major banks. Ask the sales team which lenders have already approved the project, and check whether a subvention or construction-linked plan is on offer.

Buying decision

It is a reasonable long-term hold for buyers with an eight to twelve year horizon who believe in the airport corridor. It is weak for quick flips or rental yield: possession is in 2032, the area is still maturing, and expected gross rental yield is only about 2.6% to 3.6%.

After possession, a 2 BHK could plausibly rent for about ₹40,000 to ₹55,000 a month based on today's airport-area rents. That is roughly 2.6% to 3.6% gross on a ₹1.82 Cr purchase, before maintenance and vacancy.

The distance from central Bangalore (about 40 km to MG Road), the long 2032 possession, limited social infrastructure around Doddajala today, the lack of any resale price history, and the challenge of managing a 3,460-home community well.
