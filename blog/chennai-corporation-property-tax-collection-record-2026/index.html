Chennai Corporation's Record ₹1,436 Crore Tax Haul, and the 6% Hike Homeowners Should Watch For

By the Luxura Habitat Team•October 13, 2026•6 Min Read

Greater Chennai Corporation (GCC) collected a record Rs 1,436.75 crore combined from property tax and professional tax in the first half of FY 2026-27, its highest-ever first-half figure, according to DT Next's reporting published October 2, 2026. Of that total, Rs 1,124.69 crore came from property tax specifically, a 12.95% rise year on year, representing about 93.7% of the corporation's half-year property tax target of Rs 1,200 crore.

1. What Drove the Record Collection

The number didn't come out of nowhere. In the lead-up to this half-year close, GCC had pushed property owners to clear pending dues by September 30, aiming to recover Rs 847.80 crore from more than 6 lakh property owners who hadn't yet settled their liabilities, a deadline push we covered in our earlier piece on GCC's September 30 deadline. The combination of that enforcement push and regular ongoing collections appears to have driven the first-half total to a new high, landing just shy of the corporation's full-period target with half the fiscal year still to go.

2. The 6% Hike That May Be Next

Separately, GCC is reportedly preparing a 6% increase in property tax rates, which would be its first revision since the 2022 hike. The proposal is said to be expected at an upcoming Council meeting, though available reporting doesn't confirm whether it has been formally tabled or approved yet, so this remains a proposal to track rather than a confirmed change. If it does go through, it would mark a meaningful shift after several years of unchanged rates, following closely on the heels of a record collection period that shows the corporation is already improving on compliance even before any rate increase.

3. What This Means If You Own or Are Buying in Chennai

For existing owners, the immediate takeaway is straightforward: clear any pending dues now rather than waiting, since GCC has shown it is actively pursuing non-payers, and a confirmed 6% hike would apply to whatever base amount is outstanding at that point. For prospective buyers evaluating a purchase within Chennai Corporation limits, such as at a project like Shriram Kinglife in Koyambedu, it's worth factoring a modest annual property tax increase into your long-term ownership cost assumptions, alongside registration costs covered in our broader guide to Tamil Nadu's property valuation rules.

The Bottom Line

GCC's record first-half collection shows meaningfully improved compliance, built in part on a September enforcement push, while a 6% property tax hike remains a real possibility on the horizon rather than a confirmed fact. Chennai homeowners should settle any pending dues promptly and watch for formal Council confirmation of the proposed increase.
