JLL: Chennai Housing Sales Jump 22% in Q3 2026, Ahead of Bengaluru's 18% Growth

By the Luxura Habitat Team•November 23, 2026•8 Min Read

A JLL India report released in early November 2026 found that housing sales across the country's top seven cities rose about 6% year-on-year in the first nine months of the year, to roughly 214,562 units, reversing the 12% decline recorded over the same period in 2025. The more striking number sits one level down: the value of those sales rose about 15%, more than double the unit-growth rate, because homes priced at Rs 1 crore and above grew 23% year-on-year and now account for 72% of all sales nationally, up from 62% a year earlier. Within that national picture, the two cities this site covers told genuinely different stories in the July-September quarter. Chennai posted the sharpest sales growth of any of the seven cities tracked, up 22% year-on-year, ahead of Bengaluru's still-healthy 18% and Hyderabad's 16%. Mumbai and Pune, by contrast, were the only two cities in the group to show declines over the nine-month period, down 1% and 13% respectively.

1. Chennai's 22% Is the Headline Most Buyers Will Miss

Bengaluru tends to dominate South Indian real estate coverage, so a quarter where Chennai actually outpaces it on sales growth is worth sitting with. A 22% year-on-year jump in a single quarter is a meaningfully stronger number than the kind of steady, incremental growth Chennai's market has typically been associated with, and it lines up with separate reporting this year on rising GCC office leasing and IT-driven residential demand along corridors like OMR, where projects such as Sobha Wisteria in Egattur sit. A single quarter's sales figure does not establish a trend on its own, but a 22% jump is large enough that it is unlikely to be pure noise, and it is consistent with Chennai's broader 2026 narrative of accelerating institutional and end-user interest.

2. Bengaluru's Story Is About Supply, Not Just Demand

Bengaluru's 18% sales growth in Q3 is itself a strong number, but the more distinctive fact in JLL's data is on the supply side: Bengaluru posted the single biggest jump in new housing launches among the seven cities, with overall new supply across all seven cities up 41% over the nine months and Q3 launches alone reaching roughly 90,918 units, up 28% year-on-year. That combination, sales up but new supply up by even more, is a market where developers are confident enough in demand to keep launching aggressively, which is generally healthy for buyers because it keeps choice and negotiating room from narrowing even as sales grow. It is also consistent with the sheer volume of live Bengaluru launches this site tracks across corridors like Devanahalli, Yelahanka, Hennur and Kanakapura Road.

3. Why Value Is Growing Faster Than Volume

A 6% rise in units sold against a 15% rise in sales value is not a contradiction, it is a mix shift. As more of each city's total sales come from the Rs 1 crore-plus bracket, the average transaction size rises even if the raw count of homes sold grows more modestly. This matters for how a buyer should read "the market is up" headlines: it does not necessarily mean every price band is seeing equally strong demand. A buyer shopping in the Rs 50-80 lakh range in either city should look at segment-specific data where available, rather than assuming the premium-driven national average applies evenly across all price points.

4. What This Means If You're Buying in Chennai or Bengaluru Right Now

None of this changes the price, RERA status or possession date of any specific project, and a citywide statistic should never substitute for project-level diligence. What it does offer is useful context on market temperature. In Chennai, a 22% sales jump alongside continued OMR and GCC-driven office demand suggests sustained end-user and investor interest in established corridors; in Bengaluru, an 18% sales rise paired with a 41% jump in new launches suggests a market still being actively built out rather than one running short of new supply. Buyers in both cities should still negotiate project by project: a strong citywide number does not mean every individual launch is priced fairly, and a soft citywide quarter does not mean every well-located project is a bargain.

The Bottom Line

JLL's nine-month 2026 data shows Chennai outpacing Bengaluru on sales growth for once, 22% versus 18% in Q3, inside a national market where premium homes are increasingly driving overall value growth even as unit-sales growth stays comparatively modest at 6%. For buyers in either city, the practical takeaway is that demand remains real and broadly healthy in both markets, but the premium skew means it is worth checking how your specific price band and corridor are actually performing rather than relying on the city-wide average alone.
