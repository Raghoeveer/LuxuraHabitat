Blue Line Metro Trial Runs Begin: What It Means for Brigade Eternia, Yelahanka

By the Luxura Habitat Team•November 15, 2026•8 Min Read

Bengaluru's Namma Metro Blue Line reached a real milestone in the second week of November 2026. Telangana Today reported on November 11 that trial runs had begun on the line, and Swarajya Mag reported a day earlier that kinematic test runs were expected through the month, with power already fed to the third rail and overhead cables on the Baiyappanahalli depot to Marathahalli stretch since October 30. For a metro project that has absorbed one timeline revision after another through 2026, actual trains moving under their own power is the kind of update that matters more than another ministerial statement, and it is worth unpacking exactly what it does and doesn't mean if you are watching North Bengaluru's Yelahanka corridor.

Where Yelahanka Actually Sits on the Blue Line

The Blue Line is being built in two broad stretches. The central segment runs along the Outer Ring Road through Marathahalli and KR Puram, and it is this stretch, from the Baiyappanahalli depot toward Marathahalli, where the trial and kinematic test runs reported this month are taking place. Phase 2B, the 38.44 km extension that will eventually carry the line from KR Puram through Hebbal, Yelahanka and Devanahalli to Kempegowda International Airport, is a separate and still-under-construction stretch. Business Today reported in August 2026 that the overall Blue Line project had reached about 72% construction progress, and a May 2026 report noted that Phase 2B's own timeline had already slipped from an earlier December 2026 target toward around mid-2027, with some estimates for full airport connectivity stretching toward the end of 2027. Different outlets give different months, so we are deliberately not picking a single date here. The honest summary is that Yelahanka's own stretch is behind the segment currently being tested, and is realistically a 2027 story rather than a 2026 one.

Why a Trial Run Elsewhere on the Line Still Matters

Metro lines are commissioned in stages outward from a depot, and electrification reaching live track is one of the prerequisite milestones that has to happen somewhere on a line before any stretch can be tested, let alone opened. Seeing it happen on the Baiyappanahalli to Marathahalli stretch is a credibility signal for the Blue Line programme as a whole, the kind that buyers in corridors further along the line, including Yelahanka, have been waiting years for without much visible proof of progress. It does not move Yelahanka's own opening date forward. What it does is reduce some of the execution-risk discount that buyers in under-construction metro corridors tend to apply when the entire project feels stuck on paper, because there is now something physical and verifiable to point to.

Brigade Eternia's Position in This Corridor

This is exactly the kind of update that matters for anyone currently evaluating Brigade Eternia, Brigade Group's residential development opposite the KRN Complex on Yelahanka. The project is spread across 14 acres and comprises 12 towers (2 basements plus ground plus 13 or 14 floors) housing 1,124 units in 3 and 4 BHK configurations, laid out at what Brigade describes as an intentionally low-density scale for a project of this size. The 3 BHK units range from 1,653 to 2,111 sq ft and start at Rs 2.47 crore onward on a base-price basis, while the 4 BHK units, spanning 2,714 to 2,926 sq ft, are currently priced on request. The project is RERA registered under PRM/KA/RERA/1251/309/PR/070325/007559, and Brigade's own listing shows an estimated possession in mid-2029.

On distances, Brigade's own site lists RMZ Galleria office space at about 3.5 km and Sattva Horizon at roughly 4 km, Manyata Tech Park at about 6.4 km, National Public School at just 0.6 km, REVA University at 4.3 km, Galleria Mall and Mall of Asia within 3.5 to 5.5 km, and Sparsh and Regal hospitals at 2.8 km and 4.7 km respectively. On site, the amenity plan includes a 4,500 sq ft co-working space, a learning centre, a biophilic lounge, an open-air theatre, a medical clinic, a swimming pool, badminton courts and a fully equipped gym, positioned around a central courtyard. You can review the full floor plans and master plan on the Brigade Eternia project page.

Reading the Possession Date Against the Metro Timeline

This is where the two pieces of information are genuinely worth reading together rather than separately. Brigade Eternia's own estimated possession of mid-2029 sits comfortably past even the more conservative 2027 estimates currently floating around for the Phase 2B airport line's opening. Construction delays are possible on both sides, metro projects in particular have a well-documented history of slipping further, but the gap between a mid-2029 handover and a metro line that multiple reports already place in 2027 gives this project more breathing room than most. Buyers booking into Eternia today are in a reasonable position to actually move in with the Yelahanka metro stretch operational, or close to it, rather than waiting years past possession for the metro to catch up, which has been the more common pattern in several of the city's other metro-adjacent corridors over the past decade.

What the Numbers Look Like for a Buyer

Taking the project's own starting price of Rs 2.47 crore for the smallest 3 BHK as a reference point, a buyer putting down a 20% down payment (around Rs 49.4 lakh) and financing the remaining roughly Rs 1.98 crore over a 20-year tenure at an illustrative 8.5% floating rate, broadly in line with where large-bank floating home loan rates have been sitting since the RBI's October 2026 repo rate hike to 5.50%, would be looking at an EMI in the region of Rs 1.71 lakh a month. This is a planning reference only, not a quote from any specific lender, since actual rates, processing fees and eligibility vary by bank and borrower profile, and you should get a current amortisation schedule from your bank or our team before budgeting against it.

A few practical steps are worth taking before committing. First, confirm current 4 BHK pricing and unit availability directly, since those figures were listed as on request at the time of writing. Second, check Brigade Eternia's quarterly and annual filing history on the Karnataka RERA portal using the registration number above; we've written separately about why both the quarterly disclosure deadline and the annual audit report deadline matter for buyers evaluating any under-construction project. Third, if you are comparing Yelahanka options more broadly, our existing Century Kindle vs Brigade Eternia comparison is a useful next read.

The Bottom Line

November's trial runs are real progress for the Blue Line as a programme, but they are happening on a different stretch than the one that will eventually serve Yelahanka, and anyone telling you the airport metro is "about to open" at Yelahanka specifically is getting ahead of the evidence. What the milestone does support is a reasonable degree of confidence that the broader project is moving again after a stretch of delays, which, paired with Brigade Eternia's own mid-2029 possession timeline, puts this project in a better position than most to actually deliver alongside working metro connectivity rather than years ahead of it.
