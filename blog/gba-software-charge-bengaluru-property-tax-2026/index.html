GBA's New "Software Charge" on Bengaluru Property Tax: What It Actually Costs You

By the Luxura Habitat Team•September 24, 2026•6 Min Read

Bengaluru property owners have a new line item on their annual tax bill. According to a Deccan Herald report published around September 20, 2026, the Greater Bengaluru Authority (GBA), the successor civic body to BBMP, has started levying a software charge of Rs 200 for residential properties and Rs 1,000 for commercial properties, collected in addition to the standard annual property tax. The move has already drawn political criticism, with the report quoting critics who described it as "looting in broad daylight."

1. What's Actually Being Charged

The software charge applies on top of the existing annual property tax bill, not in place of it. At Rs 200 for a residential unit, the amount itself is modest in isolation, small enough that it's unlikely to change anyone's calculations around whether to buy a specific home. The more relevant point for homeowners is that it's a new, additional line item on a bill that already includes property tax, and in some cases separate cess amounts and penalties for late payment.

2. Why This Story Is Getting Attention

The criticism reported around this charge isn't really about the Rs 200 figure itself. It reflects a broader pattern that Bengaluru property owners have watched play out over recent years: incremental new charges and fees layered onto property ownership costs, from tax revisions to the B-Khata to A-Khata conversion costs some owners have had to bear. Individually, each addition looks small. Collectively, they add up to a steadily rising cost of holding property in the city.

  • The amount is small, Rs 200 residential and Rs 1,000 commercial, per the reported figures.
  • It's mandatory and recurring, collected alongside the annual property tax rather than as a one-time fee.
  • It's part of a pattern, not an isolated event, which is why it has generated political pushback despite the modest figure involved.

3. How This Differs From GBA's Defaulter Crackdown

It's worth distinguishing this from a separate, unrelated GBA initiative: the authority's ongoing crackdown on chronic property tax defaulters, where it has warned owners with significant outstanding dues and gained powers to acquire chronically defaulting properties at guidance value when auctions fail. That crackdown targets owners who haven't paid what they already owe. This software charge, by contrast, is a new fee applied to everyone paying property tax, including owners who are fully compliant and paying on time.

4. What This Means If You Own or Are Buying Property in Bengaluru

For an existing owner, this simply means budgeting for a slightly higher annual property tax bill going forward. For a prospective buyer, it's not a factor that should influence a purchase decision on its own, given the small absolute amount, but it's a reasonable data point when thinking about the total, ongoing cost of owning property in Bengaluru rather than focusing purely on the upfront purchase price.

The Bottom Line

A Rs 200 software charge won't materially change anyone's home-buying math, but it's a useful reminder that property ownership costs in Bengaluru extend well beyond the purchase price and stamp duty, and tend to accumulate over time through exactly this kind of incremental civic charge. Budget for it, and keep an eye on your annual tax bill for further additions.
