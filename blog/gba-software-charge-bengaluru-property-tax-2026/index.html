Bengaluru's New 'Software Charge' on Property Tax: What Homeowners Should Know

By the Luxura Habitat Team•September 23, 2026•6 Min Read

Bengaluru property owners checking their latest property tax receipts this month have noticed a new line item they weren't expecting: a "software charge" of Rs 200 a year for residential properties and Rs 1,000 a year for commercial properties, added by the Greater Bengaluru Authority's five corporations. The charge appeared mid-way through the current financial year without a dedicated public notification, and it's since drawn political criticism and public pushback. Here's what it actually is, and what it means for your annual homeownership costs.

1. What the Charge Actually Covers

GBA has defended the addition by invoking a "user pays" principle: Bengaluru's civic administration now runs several of its core property-related functions, including property tax collection, e-Khata processing and related digital services, through online platforms, and maintaining that infrastructure costs money. The software charge, per GBA's own explanation, is meant to fund the development, upgrades and upkeep of these systems going forward, separately from the base property tax amount.

2. Why It's Controversial

The criticism hasn't been about the amount itself, which is modest, but about how it was introduced. The charge showed up on tax bills mid-financial-year, without a standalone announcement preceding it, which is what triggered the initial public reaction. Karnataka BJP state president B.Y. Vijayendra has publicly questioned the charge, seeking details on its legal basis, cost breakup and accountability. Residents have separately asked a fair question: since property tax is already meant to fund municipal services and administration, including the systems used to collect it, why does the software cost need a distinct line item on top?

3. The Bigger Pattern: A Year of New GBA Rules

The software charge isn't an isolated move. It lands in a year where the Greater Bengaluru Authority has rolled out several changes affecting property owners: a final-warning crackdown on chronic property tax defaulters, draft rules proposing paid residential parking permits, and, on the more buyer-friendly side, the September 21 launch of the upgraded e-Aasthi 2.0 portal for e-Khata services alongside a new B-Khata to A-Khata conversion window. Individually, none of these are large costs or dramatic policy shifts. Together, they point to a civic administration actively tightening enforcement and adding recurring charges as it modernises its digital backbone, a trend worth factoring into how you budget for Bengaluru homeownership beyond the headline property tax rate.

4. What to Actually Do About It

  • Check your latest receipt or the online portal for a separate software-charge line before your next payment cycle, so it doesn't come as a surprise.
  • Budget for it as a small, recurring annual cost, similar to how you'd account for a minor increase in the base property tax rate.
  • If you're closing on a purchase this quarter, ask your seller or the previous owner's tax records to confirm which cycle the charge first applied to, so you can settle prorated dues cleanly at registration.

The Bottom Line

Rs 200 a year is not going to change anyone's buying decision on its own, but the way it was introduced, quietly, mid-cycle, without prior notice, is a useful reminder that Bengaluru's civic charges are shifting more frequently than they used to as the city's administration digitises. If you're evaluating a Bengaluru property this year, it's worth checking the latest tax receipt rather than relying on last year's numbers.
