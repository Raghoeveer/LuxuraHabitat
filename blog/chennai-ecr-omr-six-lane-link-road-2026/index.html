Chennai's Proposed Six-Lane ECR-OMR Link Road: What It Means for Southern Corridor Buyers

By the Luxura Habitat Team•November 27, 2026•8 Min Read

Tamil Nadu's Highways Department has invited tenders for a detailed project report on a proposed six-lane link road connecting East Coast Road (ECR) and Old Mahabalipuram Road (OMR) in Chennai's southern growth corridor. The consultant that wins the tender will have three months to complete the DPR. It is an early-stage procedural step, not a construction announcement, but it is a genuine, dated development worth understanding if you own or are evaluating property anywhere along OMR's southern stretch, because it points to where the state sees a real bottleneck.

1. Why This Stretch Needs a Connector

The Thoraipakkam-Neelankarai road is currently the only direct connection between OMR and ECR along this part of the southern corridor. That forces anyone travelling from the Sholinganallur, Siruseri or Kelambakkam stretch of OMR, home to much of Chennai's IT corridor workforce, toward Kovalam or Mahabalipuram on ECR into a detour of around 12 km. A six-lane link further down the corridor would give residents and commuters a second, more direct crossing option, reducing dependence on that single existing road.

2. This Is Different From the Neelankarai Steel Bridge Already Under Construction

Buyers researching this topic should be careful not to conflate two separate projects. Already underway, and further along, is a smaller link road and steel bridge, roughly 1.4 to 1.5 km, crossing the Buckingham Canal, connecting the OMR-Pallavaram Thoraipakkam Radial Road junction to ECR at Neelankarai. Coastal regulators have cleared this bridge, estimated at around Rs 204 crore, for Coastal Regulation Zone requirements, and by one account roughly 800 to 860 metres of roadwork had been completed on the OMR side, with about 600 metres still to be laid on the ECR side. Officials have said this specific link could divert about 40 percent of traffic away from the OMR-Pallavaram Thoraipakkam Radial Road junction once it opens. The new six-lane proposal, by contrast, is a larger project further south along the corridor, still only at the DPR tender stage. Both matter, but they are not the same project, and they are at very different stages of readiness.

3. A Third Piece: Grade Separators at Perungudi and SRP Tools

Rounding out the picture, Chennai Metro Rail Limited was awarded a contract of roughly Rs 1,138 crore for grade separators at the Perungudi and SRP Tools junctions on OMR, intended to ease congestion at two of the corridor's busiest intersections, with the contract signed around March 2026 and an 18-month completion window from commencement. None of these three projects, the new DPR tender, the Neelankarai bridge, or the Perungudi and SRP Tools grade separators, will single-handedly transform OMR's traffic situation, but together they describe a corridor the state is actively trying to decongest from multiple angles at once.

4. What This Means If You Own or Are Buying on OMR

For buyers evaluating projects along the OMR and ECR corridors, this is useful directional context rather than a reason to act immediately. A DPR tender is one of the earliest stages of any road project, commonly followed by land acquisition, environmental and coastal clearances, final design, funding allocation and tendering for construction, each of which can take months or years and each of which can also stall. The Neelankarai bridge's own history, cleared for CRZ requirements but still incomplete on the ECR side as of the most recent reporting, is a useful reminder that even a project with clearances in hand can take years to finish.

Several residential developments sit within this broader southern OMR-ECR growth corridor, including Godrej Azure in Padur, Radiance Edgewood in Navalur, and Sobha Wisteria in Egattur. None of these projects' prices or possession timelines are affected by a DPR tender, and we would not treat this news as a reason to pay a premium today. It is, however, a reasonable factor to weigh for anyone taking a multi-year view of the corridor's connectivity, alongside each project's own fundamentals, builder track record and pricing relative to comparable OMR options.

The Bottom Line

Chennai's southern corridor is seeing real, if staggered, road investment: a DPR tender for a new six-lane ECR-OMR link, an existing steel bridge project at Neelankarai that is most of the way to completion on one side and behind on the other, and grade separators coming at two congested OMR junctions. Each is at a different stage of maturity, and none should be treated as settled until land, funding and construction timelines are confirmed.
