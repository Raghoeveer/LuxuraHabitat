Bengaluru Leads India in Housing Price Growth, Up 14.5% in Q3 2026

By the Luxura Habitat Team•October 31, 2026•6 Min Read

Bengaluru recorded the highest year-on-year housing price growth among India's six major residential markets in the July-September 2026 quarter, at 14.5%, according to a report from NoBroker covered by Aurum PropTech's Pulse platform on October 26, 2026. That puts Bengaluru ahead of NCR at 10.6%, Chennai at 10.2%, MMR at 8.9%, Pune at 7.7% and Hyderabad at 5.6%, making it the only one of the six markets to cross the 14% mark this quarter.

1. Price Growth, Not Sales Growth, Is the Headline Here

It's worth being precise about what this number actually measures, because we've separately covered a different Q3 2026 data point that can easily be confused with it. PropEquity's Q3 2026 sales figures, which we covered in our piece on Bengaluru and Chennai's Q3 housing sales, showed Bengaluru's unit sales volume rose only marginally, about 1% year on year. This NoBroker report measures something different: how much per-sq-ft prices rose year on year, not how many homes changed hands. A market can post flat-to-modest sales volume growth while prices still climb meaningfully, when demand is concentrated enough, or supply tight enough, in the right micro-markets, and that appears to be exactly what's playing out in Bengaluru this quarter.

2. Is This a Sign of Overheating?

A PropNewz analysis published October 9, 2026 took on a version of this exact question after a viral video claimed Bengaluru's market was heading for a crash. Drawing on Knight Frank's January-June 2026 data and Anarock's Q3 2026 release, the piece concluded there is no crash underway, since a genuine crash requires falling sales and falling prices happening together, and Bengaluru was showing neither. It did flag one metric worth watching: the price-to-rent ratio in South Bengaluru, which has been climbing and is the kind of indicator that typically moves ahead of any genuine correction. Strong, broad-based price growth alongside steady-to-growing sales, rather than falling sales, is a materially healthier pattern than the one that usually precedes a downturn.

3. What This Means If You're Buying Right Now

For buyers already decided on a Bengaluru purchase, a city leading the country in price growth is generally a reason to move rather than wait, since pre-launch and under-construction pricing at new projects tends to track the broader market over time rather than move independently of it. That's relevant context for anyone evaluating a pre-launch project like Brigade Silvercrest on Kanakapura Road, where current indicative pricing could look different once the project moves from pre-launch to a formal RERA-registered launch. That said, a city-wide average of 14.5% hides large differences between corridors, so it's worth getting a current price-per-sq-ft comparison for your specific micro-market rather than anchoring only on the headline number.

The Bottom Line

Bengaluru's housing market closed Q3 2026 as the country's price-growth leader among six major cities, a genuinely different and complementary data point to the relatively flat sales-volume figures we've covered separately. Rising prices alongside steady sales, rather than falling sales, is the more reassuring combination for anyone worried about a bubble, though South Bengaluru's price-to-rent ratio remains the metric to keep an eye on.
