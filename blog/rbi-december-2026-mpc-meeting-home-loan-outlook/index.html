RBI's December 2026 MPC Meeting: What Bengaluru and Chennai Homebuyers Should Expect

By the Luxura Habitat Team•November 17, 2026•6 Min Read

The Reserve Bank of India's Monetary Policy Committee meets next on December 2-4, 2026, its first review since the October 7 meeting raised the repo rate by 25 basis points to 5.50% and shifted the central bank's stance to "calibrated tightening." Unusually for a rate-setting meeting this far out, economists are already split on what comes next, rather than converging on a consensus hold. SBI Research has reportedly retained its call for a larger, 50-basis-point hike in December, citing persistent inflationary pressure and elevated crude oil prices, while Nomura expects a more modest 25-basis-point move, to 5.75%. A third camp expects the RBI to pause altogether, pointing to low headline inflation and resilient GDP growth as reasons not to tighten further so soon after October's increase. For Bengaluru and Chennai homebuyers carrying or considering a floating-rate home loan, this is the kind of split-decision setup worth preparing for in advance, rather than reacting to after the announcement.

1. Why the Forecasts Disagree So Sharply

The disagreement among economists isn't noise; it reflects a genuine tension in the data the RBI will be weighing. On one hand, headline inflation readings have stayed relatively contained through much of 2026, and GDP growth has held up well enough that some analysts see no urgent case for further tightening so soon after the October hike. On the other hand, elevated crude oil prices feed directly into transport and input costs across the economy, and a projection of headline inflation near 6% for Q3 FY2026-27 gives the hawkish camp, including SBI Research's 50-basis-point call, a reasonable basis for expecting the RBI to act more aggressively rather than wait. When credible forecasters disagree this much heading into a policy meeting, the honest takeaway for a homebuyer is that the outcome is genuinely uncertain, not that one side is obviously right.

2. What Each Scenario Would Mean for a Typical Home Loan

The practical impact scales with the size of any hike. On an illustrative Rs 75 lakh loan over 20 years, a 25-basis-point increase has previously added roughly Rs 1,100-1,200 to the monthly EMI, or extended the tenure by about a year if the EMI itself is held flat. A 50-basis-point move, the scenario SBI Research is reportedly still pricing in, would roughly double that impact, pushing a similar loan's EMI up by over Rs 2,000 a month, or adding closer to two years of tenure if monthly payments stay unchanged. A pause, by contrast, would leave existing floating-rate EMIs untouched for at least one more review cycle. Buyers currently comparing a fixed-rate offer against a floating-rate one, or deciding whether to lock in a rate now ahead of a possible increase, are the group most directly affected by which of these three scenarios plays out on December 4.

  • RBI rules require lenders to offer a reset option. If you already hold a floating-rate loan, you are entitled to periodically choose between a revised floating rate, a fixed-rate conversion, or a change in tenure versus EMI; it's worth reviewing that option with your lender regardless of which way December's decision goes.
  • Fixed-rate loans are insulated from this specific decision. Their rate and EMI stay constant through the loan tenure once locked in, which is why some buyers facing uncertainty ahead of a policy meeting choose to lock in a fixed rate rather than wait.
  • A pause doesn't mean rates are done rising for good. Even if the RBI holds in December, the "calibrated tightening" stance adopted in October signals the central bank hasn't ruled out further increases at subsequent meetings if inflation data doesn't cooperate.

3. What Bengaluru and Chennai Buyers Should Actually Do Before December 4

For anyone close to finalising a home loan, whether for a ready-to-move apartment in Bengaluru or a new-launch project along Chennai's OMR corridor, the sensible approach is to get your loan paperwork and rate comparisons in order before the meeting rather than after. If you're weighing a specific project, say a mid-segment North Bengaluru apartment or a premium OMR development, run the EMI math at both today's 5.50% repo-linked rate and at a half-point higher, so a December surprise doesn't change your underlying affordability decision. If you already hold a floating-rate loan and have built up savings, this is also a reasonable window to consider a partial prepayment ahead of a possible rate increase, since reducing principal before a hike lowers the base on which any new, higher rate gets applied. None of this requires predicting which of the three scenarios actually happens; it just means your numbers won't be caught off guard by whichever one does.

The Bottom Line

Economists are unusually split heading into the RBI's December 2-4, 2026 policy meeting, with credible calls ranging from a 50-basis-point hike to a full pause, following October's 25-basis-point increase to 5.50%. Rather than guessing which call is right, Bengaluru and Chennai homebuyers are better served preparing their EMI math for a range of outcomes, reviewing their lender's reset options if they already hold a floating-rate loan, and treating a fixed-rate quote as a genuine hedge if rate uncertainty is making a purchase decision harder than it should be.
