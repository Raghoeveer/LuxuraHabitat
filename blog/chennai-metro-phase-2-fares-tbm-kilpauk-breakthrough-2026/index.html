Chennai Metro Phase 2: Fares Fixed at Rs 10-40, and a Second Line Hits a Milestone at Kilpauk

By the Luxura Habitat Team•October 1, 2026•7 Min Read

Chennai Metro Phase 2 had two separate pieces of news land within 48 hours of each other at the end of September. First, fares for the soon-to-open Poonamallee Bypass-Vadapalani corridor were fixed between Rs 10 and Rs 40 on 29 September 2026, ahead of its planned 11 October inauguration. Then, on 30 September, L&T's tunnel boring machine "Kaveri" completed a 685-metre underground drive and broke through at Kilpauk Medical College Station, a milestone on an entirely different stretch of Phase 2, Corridor 3 (Line 3), which runs between Kellys and Taramani Road Junction. Together, the two updates show a project advancing on multiple fronts at once rather than progress concentrated around a single opening date.

1. What the New Fares Tell Buyers

A Rs 10-40 fare band for the Poonamallee-Vadapalani stretch puts Chennai Metro's new corridor broadly in line with typical Indian metro pricing for a comparable distance, and it's a concrete, budgetable number for anyone weighing a daily commute from this corridor into the city. For West Chennai residents evaluating a purchase along Poonamallee High Road, Porur or Vadapalani, having an actual fare structure, not just a connectivity promise, makes it easier to compare total cost of ownership between a car-dependent commute and a metro-linked one.

2. Why the Kilpauk Breakthrough Is a Separate, Meaningful Story

It's worth being precise about what's progressing where. The October 11 opening covers the western Poonamallee-Vadapalani stretch; the Kilpauk milestone sits on Line 3, the 45.8 km corridor connecting Kellys and Taramani Road Junction through central Chennai neighbourhoods including Chetpet, Kilpauk, Egmore and on towards Adyar and Taramani. TBM Kaveri's breakthrough at Kilpauk Medical College Station is tunnelling progress on this separate line, which doesn't yet have a confirmed public opening date. For residents and buyers along this central corridor, it's a reminder that Phase 2 isn't a single switch that flips in October, it's a multi-year, multi-line build-out, and construction milestones on lines without a near-term opening date are still meaningful markers of overall project momentum.

  • West Chennai gets a dated, budgetable commute cost. The Rs 10-40 fare band removes one unknown for anyone finalising a purchase decision along the Poonamallee-Vadapalani corridor ahead of its October opening.
  • Central Chennai's Line 3 is moving in parallel, not waiting its turn. The Kilpauk breakthrough shows tunnelling work continuing on a second major corridor simultaneously, which is a reasonable signal of sustained execution capacity on Phase 2 overall.
  • Different corridors, different buyer timelines. Buyers near the Poonamallee-Vadapalani stretch can factor an October 2026 connectivity date into their decision now; buyers along Line 3's path should treat this as positive momentum without an immediate completion date attached.

The Bottom Line

Chennai Metro Phase 2 is clearly in an active execution phase across more than one corridor at once: a dated opening with real fares for West Chennai, and continued tunnelling progress on a separate central-Chennai line that will take longer to complete. For buyers, the practical read is to track your specific corridor's own timeline rather than treating "Chennai Metro Phase 2" as a single event, since different stretches of this project are moving at different speeds and will reach completion on different schedules.
