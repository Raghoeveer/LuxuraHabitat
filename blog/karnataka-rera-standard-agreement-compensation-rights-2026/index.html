K-RERA: Signing a Builder's Standard Agreement Doesn't Waive Your Compensation Rights

By the Luxura Habitat Team•September 22, 2026•6 Min Read

Karnataka's RERA case digest for the week of September 14 to 20, 2026 carries a ruling that deserves more attention than it's getting: the Karnataka Real Estate Regulatory Authority has held that homebuyers do not lose their statutory right to compensation for delayed possession merely because they signed a standard-form Agreement for Sale drafted by the builder. If you've ever signed a builder's agreement without being able to negotiate a single clause, and most buyers have, this ruling is directly about you.

1. What K-RERA Actually Held

The authority found that a standard-form Agreement for Sale, one prepared entirely by the promoter with no real room for the buyer to negotiate or alter its terms, cannot be used to defeat a buyer's statutory protection under the Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016. K-RERA Chairman Rakesh Singh was reported as observing that allottees who enter into such an agreement "cannot be presumed to have voluntarily waived or surrendered their statutory rights merely by signing the said Agreement."

2. Why This Is Bigger Than One Case

Nearly every builder in Bengaluru uses a pre-printed, non-negotiable Agreement for Sale. Buyers sign what's handed to them; there is rarely a genuine back-and-forth over individual clauses, especially in a high-demand project where a builder can simply move on to the next buyer. Some of these standard agreements include language that appears to cap, limit, or otherwise dilute a buyer's right to compensation if possession is delayed. This ruling draws a clear line: a clause buried in a document you had no real ability to negotiate doesn't get to override a right the law gives you directly.

3. What Compensation Actually Looks Like Under RERA

When a builder misses the possession date committed in the registered project, RERA gives a buyer two broad options:

  • Exit the project. Withdraw and claim a full refund of the amount paid, along with interest, if you no longer want to wait for the project.
  • Stay invested and claim delay interest. Continue in the project and claim interest for every month of delay until the builder actually hands over possession.

This ruling doesn't change which of these options exists. What it changes is a builder's ability to argue that a buyer signed away access to either option simply by putting a signature on the standard agreement.

4. What This Doesn't Mean

It's worth being precise here: this ruling is about whether signing a standard-form agreement itself waives your compensation rights, not a blanket guarantee that every delay claim automatically succeeds. A buyer still needs to establish the committed possession date under the project's RERA registration and show it was missed, typically by filing a formal complaint with K-RERA. What the ruling removes is a builder's ability to point to boilerplate contract language as a reason your claim shouldn't even be heard.

5. What to Check in Your Own Agreement

  1. The exact possession date committed in your Agreement for Sale, and whether it matches the date listed on the project's Karnataka RERA registration.
  2. The delay interest rate stated in the agreement, which should track the rate prescribed under RERA rules rather than a builder-favourable figure.
  3. Force majeure clauses that could be used to justify an extension, and how broadly they're worded.
  4. Any clause that reads like a waiver of your right to claim compensation or interest, since this ruling suggests such a clause is unlikely to hold up on its own if challenged.

The Bottom Line

This ruling is a reminder that a builder's paperwork sets the starting point for a negotiation, not the final word on your rights. If you're currently facing a possession delay on a Bengaluru project, or reviewing an Agreement for Sale before signing one, it's worth getting the actual clauses checked rather than assuming a standard document means standard, limited rights.
