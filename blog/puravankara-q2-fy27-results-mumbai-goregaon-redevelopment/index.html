Puravankara Q2 FY27: Pre-Sales Jump 62% to ₹2,136 Crore

By the Luxura Habitat Team•October 19, 2026•6 Min Read

Puravankara released its Q2 FY27 (July-September 2026) business update on October 13, 2026, reporting pre-sales of Rs 2,136 crore, up 62% year-on-year from Rs 1,322 crore in the same quarter last year, according to a Free Press Journal report. Customer collections rose 24% to Rs 1,301 crore. Alongside the numbers, the company disclosed a new Mumbai redevelopment deal and the completed sale of its commercial asset, Purva Zentech.

1. The Numbers: Pre-Sales Accelerate to 62% Growth

A 62% year-on-year jump in quarterly pre-sales is a meaningfully stronger number than what the company posted in Q1 FY27, when pre-sales had grown 28% year-on-year as the business returned to profit, a quarter we covered in detail in our earlier piece on Puravankara's Q1 FY27 results. Collections growing 24% alongside the sales number is a reasonably healthy sign too, since it suggests the booking growth is converting into actual cash coming in rather than sitting as receivables.

2. Mumbai Redevelopment: A New Asset-Light Push

The company took on redevelopment rights for three housing societies in Goregaon West, Mumbai, with an estimated gross development value of around Rs 2,600 crore. Redevelopment deals like this are typically asset-light compared to outright land purchases, since the developer works with an existing plot and an existing resident society rather than acquiring land outright. This comes in the same broad window as Puravankara's first Delhi-NCR entry in Greater Noida, which we covered separately, and its Mandur, Bengaluru land acquisition from earlier in the year. Taken together, the pattern is a developer actively deploying capital into new geographies and deal structures while its core Bengaluru business keeps selling.

3. Cashing Out of Commercial: The Purva Zentech Sale

Puravankara also completed the sale of its commercial property, Purva Zentech, on September 30, 2026, to an entity backed by ICICI Prudential AMC, at an enterprise value of Rs 625.94 crore. The transaction was executed by transferring the company's entire shareholding in Purva Ruby Properties Private Limited. Selling a mature, cash-generating commercial asset to an institutional buyer is a fairly standard capital-recycling move for a listed residential developer: it converts a long-term leased asset into immediate capital that can be redeployed into new launches, land acquisitions or redevelopment deals like the Goregaon one above, rather than tying up the balance sheet in a single commercial property indefinitely.

4. What It Means If You're Evaluating Puravankara in Bengaluru

None of this quarter's corporate activity, the Mumbai redevelopment deal or the Zentech sale, changes the pricing, configurations or possession timelines of Puravankara's existing, RERA-registered Bengaluru projects. What it does offer is a reasonably current read on the company's financial health and execution momentum if you're evaluating a project like Purva Northern Lights in Bagalur, a RERA-approved, new-launch project in the KIADB Aerospace Park corridor. A developer posting accelerating pre-sales growth and actively recycling capital into new deals is generally a healthier signal than one standing still, though it's still worth verifying a specific project's own RERA filing and construction progress independently rather than relying on parent-company results alone.

The Bottom Line

Puravankara's Q2 FY27 shows a developer whose pre-sales growth accelerated rather than cooled off, backed by a new Mumbai redevelopment deal and the clean exit of a commercial asset at a healthy valuation. For Bengaluru buyers, the more useful takeaway is simply that the company behind projects like Purva Northern Lights is showing strengthening, not weakening, business momentum this year.
