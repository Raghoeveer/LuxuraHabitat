Karnataka RERA Orders Rs 10 Lakh Payout Over Missing Amenities: What It Means for Bengaluru Buyers

By the Luxura Habitat Team•September 17, 2026•7 Min Read

Karnataka's Real Estate Regulatory Authority (K-RERA), in an order dated September 4, 2026, directed a Bengaluru builder to pay Rs 10 lakh in compensation to four homebuyers after amenities promised in the project's sales brochure, allotment letters and agreements were never delivered. The complaint was brought by four buyers from R.T. Nagar, who had each purchased flats in a project at Jala Hobli in Bengaluru North back in 2022 for around Rs 2.08 crore. The amenities in question, including a senior citizen park, open gym, spa, amphitheatre and sports facilities, were showcased prominently in the project's marketing material but were missing when the buyers went looking for them. K-RERA ordered the compensation to be paid within 60 days, with interest kicking in if it isn't.

1. Why This Order Matters Beyond This One Project

What makes this ruling significant isn't the Rs 10 lakh figure itself, it's the reasoning K-RERA used to get there. The authority observed that the builder had presented buyers with, in effect, "colourful dreams" through the brochure, and held that amenities shown in marketing material, allotment letters and the sale agreement are binding representations, not optional flourishes a developer can quietly drop during construction. Crucially, this obligation doesn't evaporate once the sale deed is registered. For homebuyers, that's an important point of leverage: the brochure you were shown at booking, and the annexures attached to your agreement, carry real legal weight if the builder later cuts corners on what was promised.

2. The Pattern RERA Orders Like This Reveal

This isn't an isolated incident. RERA authorities across states, including Karnataka's, have increasingly used their powers to hold developers to their marketing claims rather than treating amenity shortfalls as a private grievance between buyer and builder. For a market like Bengaluru, where large gated communities routinely market clubhouses, sports courts, senior citizen zones and landscaped open spaces as core selling points, this sets a clear expectation: what's in the brochure needs to show up on the ground, on the promised amenity list, in the promised specifications, and by the promised timeline.

  • Brochures aren't just marketing. Once amenities are specified in the brochure, allotment letter or agreement, they become part of what a builder is contractually expected to deliver.
  • The sale deed doesn't close the door. Buyers who've already registered their property can still pursue a complaint if promised amenities are missing or incomplete.
  • Compensation isn't the only remedy. RERA authorities can also direct specific performance, meaning ordering the builder to actually build the missing amenity, depending on the facts of the case.

3. What This Means If You're Evaluating a Project Right Now

If you're currently shortlisting an apartment or villa project in Bengaluru, this order is a useful reminder to treat the amenity list as seriously as the price and floor plan. Ask for the amenities to be itemised clearly in your agreement, not just referenced vaguely as "as per brochure," and cross-check the brochure's promises against the project's RERA registration filing, which is a public document on the Karnataka RERA portal (rera.karnataka.gov.in). If a builder is reluctant to specify amenity details in writing, that reluctance is itself useful information.

4. If an Amenity Is Missing on a Project You've Already Booked

Start by documenting the gap in writing: photograph the current state of the amenity area, compare it against the brochure and any master plan you were given, and raise it formally with the builder's customer relations team with a clear timeline for a response. If the builder doesn't resolve it satisfactorily, Karnataka RERA's complaint process is designed for exactly this kind of dispute, and as this September 2026 order shows, the authority is willing to award compensation, and go further, when a developer's promises and its delivery don't match.

The Bottom Line

This K-RERA order is a reminder that the fine print in your project brochure isn't decorative, it's enforceable. Whether you're evaluating a new booking or living in a project where the amenities didn't fully materialise, it's worth checking your paperwork against what's actually on the ground.
