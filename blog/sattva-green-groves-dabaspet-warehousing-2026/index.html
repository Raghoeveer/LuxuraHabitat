Sattva Group's Rs 700 Crore Dabaspet Warehousing Bet: What It Means for Sattva Green Groves Buyers

By the Luxura Habitat Team•November 23, 2026•10 Min Read

Reports that surfaced on November 6 and 7, 2026, citing people familiar with the matter (Sattva Group's managing director reportedly declined to comment when asked), said the group has quietly acquired around 200 acres of land near Dabaspet and Narsapura, two points on the Tumkur Road and NH-48 corridor northwest of Bengaluru, to build warehousing and industrial facilities. The reported development potential is about five million sq ft, with an initial investment of roughly Rs 700 crore funded through internal accruals and construction finance. According to the same reports, this marks Sattva Group's first move into industrial and logistics real estate within Karnataka, part of a stated ambition to build a chain of logistics parks across India, starting in the south, backed by a planned Rs 12,000-14,000 crore of investment over the next two to three years. It is worth being upfront about sourcing here: the figures come from unnamed sources rather than an official company statement, so treat the exact numbers as reported rather than confirmed until Sattva Group itself puts out a release or regulatory filing.

What makes this relevant to this site's readers is geography. Dabaspet sits only about 10 to 12 km further up the same Tumkur Road corridor from Nelamangala, the town where Sattva Group already markets Sattva Green Groves, a 45-acre, 735-plot residential layout. A developer putting fresh capital into industrial land a short drive from its own residential project, on the same highway, is a combination worth unpacking on its own terms, separating what is genuinely useful signal for a plot buyer from what is simply two unrelated transactions sharing a postcode.

1. What the Reported Deal Actually Involves

Per the available reporting, the roughly 200-acre parcel spans two locations, Dabaspet and Narsapura, both established industrial nodes along the Tumkur Road/NH-48 stretch that has hosted manufacturing and logistics activity for Bengaluru for decades, well before the city's newer growth corridors around the airport or Sarjapur Road existed. The reported Rs 700 crore initial outlay, if accurate, would fund the first phase of warehousing construction, with Sattva positioning the site as part of a broader national logistics push that starts in South India before, reportedly, expanding to other markets. None of this has yet been confirmed through an official Sattva Group press release, an exchange filing, or a land registration record that this site has independently verified, so the prudent read is that this is a credible, multiply-sourced report of an emerging business line rather than a closed, audited transaction.

2. Why a Developer's Industrial Bet Is Relevant to Its Own Residential Buyers

A developer does not deploy Rs 700 crore of fresh capital, even reportedly, without some confidence in its own balance sheet and cash flow. For a buyer evaluating a live Sattva Group project like Sattva Green Groves, a credible report of the group diversifying into warehousing on this scale is a loosely supportive, if indirect, signal about the developer's financial standing, in the same way a Q2 results announcement or a construction-progress update at one project is sometimes a useful proxy for a developer's overall health when evaluating another project from the same group. It does not substitute for checking Sattva Green Groves' own RERA filing and construction updates directly, and it should not be read as a guarantee of anything specific to Green Groves. But a developer with the balance sheet to fund a 200-acre, multi-location industrial land buy is, on balance, a more reassuring counterparty than one that is visibly cash-constrained.

  • Reported scale: ~200 acres across Dabaspet and Narsapura, ~5 million sq ft development potential, ~Rs 700 crore initial investment.
  • Funding: Internal accruals plus construction finance, per sourced reports, not a fresh equity or debt raise.
  • Proximity: Dabaspet sits roughly 10-12 km from Nelamangala, on the same Tumkur Road/NH-48 corridor as Sattva Green Groves.

3. Nelamangala's Changing Character: From Toll-Booth Town to Industrial Corridor

Sattva Green Groves' own project listing already notes "at least 60 companies within a 6 km radius," describing Nelamangala as an industrial hub in its own right, independent of this new warehousing report. A fresh, large-scale industrial investment a short distance up the same highway reinforces that existing characterisation rather than introducing a new one: Nelamangala and the stretch toward Dabaspet have functioned as an employment and logistics corridor for Bengaluru's northwest for years, and continued investment here, whichever developer is behind it, is generally consistent with sustained demand for housing, including plots, from the workforce such facilities employ. This is also a useful moment to note that Sattva is not the only developer with a plotted layout in this immediate stretch; readers comparing options directly across the corridor can see how Green Groves stacks up against a nearby alternative in our comparison of Sattva Green Groves and KNS Samooha, both plotted developments serving the same broad Nelamangala catchment.

4. What Sattva Green Groves Itself Actually Offers

Stepping away from the warehousing headlines, Sattva Green Groves is a RERA-registered plotted development spanning approximately 45 acres with 735 numbered, ready-to-build plots, located about 500 metres off Tumkur Road and close to the Nelamangala Toll Booth, the entry point into Bengaluru from this corridor. The master plan offers 20x30 ft (600 sq ft), 30x40 ft (1,200 sq ft) and 30x50 ft (1,500 sq ft) plots, along with odd sites whose dimensions vary by plot number, laid out around 17 individually named parks, a 25,000 sq ft clubhouse with a swimming pool, gym, party hall, library, indoor games and sports courts, and a cricket practice pitch, all developed to BMRDA and NPA norms. Sattva Group publishes monthly construction updates on its own project page (the latest tracked as of September 2026) but discloses no fixed handover date, describing these as ready-to-build plots rather than a possession-bound apartment project; buyers should ask for their specific plot's handover and registration date in writing. On price, the project carries no published figure; Sattva Group shares pricing only on request, so ask for a dated, written price sheet for your plot size and number before proceeding. The project's RERA registration number is PRM/KA/RERA/1250/307/PR/040524/006863, which buyers should verify independently on the Karnataka RERA portal.

5. What to Verify Before Treating Industrial Growth as a Price Guarantee

It bears repeating that land and plot appreciation is never guaranteed, a point Sattva Group's own materials make explicitly, and a nearby industrial investment, however large, does not by itself lock in future plot value at Green Groves. What it reasonably supports is a thesis: this corridor is seeing continued employment-generating investment, which tends to support housing and land demand over time, but the pace and extent of any price appreciation depends on dozens of other factors, from broader Bengaluru real estate cycles to how quickly Dabaspet's own warehousing actually gets built and leased. Buyers should independently confirm the warehousing report's progress (through Sattva's own disclosures or local land records) before treating it as settled fact, and should separately verify Green Groves' own RERA status, plot availability and price directly with the developer rather than relying on any secondary analysis, including this one.

The Bottom Line

A reported Rs 700 crore warehousing investment at Dabaspet does not change anything contractual about Sattva Green Groves, its price remains on request, its RERA registration and plot sizes are unchanged, and its possession terms are whatever the developer confirms in writing for your specific plot. What it adds is useful context: a credible report of the group behind Green Groves deploying meaningful fresh capital into an industrial corridor a short drive from its own Nelamangala layout, in a location the project's own marketing already describes as an employment hub. That is a reasonable, incremental data point for anyone evaluating this corridor, not a reason to rush a decision.
